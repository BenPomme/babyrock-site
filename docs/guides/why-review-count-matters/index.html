Guide · Google reviews

Why the number of Google reviews matters, not only the stars

A listing with dozens of recent reviews and a 4.5 often persuades more than a 4.9 with eight opinions from 2019. Customers and Google both look at how alive the page is.

Why it matters

The star average moves slowly. The count, and the date of the last review, are visible in a second. Independents live on that more than chains do. A vendor study on millions of Google reviews (SOCi) found that listings with more reviews also converted more of the people who saw them. That is a correlation from a vendor study, not a promise for your shop.

You do not choose who writes. You do choose whether the listing looks current.

Reply to what is already there. That habit, on its own, is associated in research with more reviews arriving later, because people see that someone is home.

Ask fairly for new ones: the same QR, the same sentence, no prize. Volume that comes only from 5-star guests is the volume Google does not want.

A single 2-star from last month, sitting alone as the only recent text, does more damage than the same score in a page that also has this week’s notes. More reviews will not save a bad plate. They will stop one bad afternoon from being the whole story a stranger reads.

Look at your listing the way a new customer would: last review date, last reply, mix of scores. If the last public word is from last year, that is the job for this week.
