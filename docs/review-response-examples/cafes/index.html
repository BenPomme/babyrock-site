The short answer

A good café review reply thanks the customer, names the specific thing they mentioned (the flat white, the queue, the laptop rule), owns what was yours, explains a policy once without lecturing, and invites them back. Keep it to three or four sentences and take allergen concerns offline. These 14 examples show how.

What makes café reviews different

Café reviews are short, frequent and often about small things: a lukewarm latte, a table taken by someone with a laptop, a queue that moved slowly at nine. Each one is minor on its own, but a café lives on regulars and passing trade, and a page of unanswered one-star comments tells the next person to walk on by.

  • Taste is personal. Bitter, weak or too hot for one person is perfect for another. Never tell a customer they're wrong about their coffee. Offer to remake it.
  • Policies cause most of the friction. Laptop rules, card-only payment, no-table-for-one at lunch, milk charges. Explain them once, kindly, and say where you've put them in your profile.
  • The rush is the weak spot. Most one-star reviews describe the 8.30 to 9.30 rush. If your reply names that hour and what you've changed, readers believe you.
  • Allergens and dogs are serious. Allergens belong in a private conversation. Dogs split opinion, and assistance dogs have legal protection.

Google's own page on replying to reviews says replies are public and follow its content policies. Beyond that, it's down to tone, and the examples below show it.

Sign with your first name and your role ("Hana, owner"). A café is personal, and customers like knowing who runs the place.

Coffee, queues and service

Most reviews of a café involve the drink or the wait. The honest reply names the likely cause and offers a fix you can actually deliver.

Example · Bitter coffee · independent café, Brighton

Review

Flat white tasted burnt and bitter. Asked for a fresh one and the barista rolled their eyes. Won't be back.

Reply

Thank you for telling us, and I'm sorry about the eye-roll as much as the coffee. A burnt flat white usually means the grinder needs adjusting after a busy start, and I've asked the team to taste-check the first shot each morning. If you'd like to try again, ask for me and I'll make it myself. Hana, owner

Why it works: Takes the attitude complaint seriously, names a plausible cause and a specific habit.

Example · Lukewarm drink · café, Sheffield

Review

Ordered a latte, waited ten minutes and it was barely warm. Staff were nice, but a warm coffee is the one thing a café needs to get right.

Reply

You're right, and thank you for the kind words about the team. A latte should be hot when it reaches you. The machine's steam wand was struggling on Saturday and we've had it serviced since. I'm sorry for the wait and the lukewarm drink. Please come back and I'll make sure the next one is spot on. Ben, owner

Why it works: Agrees without grovelling, explains the cause and says what was done about it.

Example · Queue at the rush · bakery café, Leeds

Review

Queue out of the door at 9am and only one person on the till. Took 25 minutes to get a coffee. Not sure it's worth it.

Reply

I'm sorry about the wait. Between 8.30 and 9.30 we're at our busiest and on Thursday we were a person short, which is on me. I've moved a second till person onto those hours, and you can order ahead on our website to skip the line. Hope you'll give us another go. Sara, manager

Why it works: Names the rush hours, takes ownership and gives a practical workaround.

Example · Wrong order · coffee kiosk, Glasgow

Review

Ordered a decaf oat cappuccino and was handed a regular one. Really worrying. Check your orders!

Reply

Thank you for telling us, and I'm very sorry about the mix-up. An order mix-up is never acceptable and I've asked the team to read the cup back to every customer. I'd like to understand what happened, so please contact me on [email]. Kenny, owner

Why it works: Short, serious, and takes the detail offline. It avoids discussing health.

Rules: laptops, Wi-Fi, card-only and seating

Policies are where good cafés lose stars. The trick is to explain the reason and make sure the policy is on your page, so the next customer can't be surprised.

Example · Laptop rules · café, Manchester

Review

Told to close my laptop at 12.30. Had ordered two coffees and a sandwich. Felt like I was being thrown out.

Reply

Thanks for telling us, and I'm sorry for how it felt. Over lunch we ask people to keep laptops away from the busiest tables so everyone can sit down, and we should have said so clearly when you ordered. I've added our laptop hours to our profile and the menu board. Please message me and I'll explain more. Nadia, owner

Why it works: Gives the reason, owns the poor explanation and fixes the information.

Example · Wi-Fi not working · café, Bristol

Review

Advertised Wi-Fi but it kept dropping and the code on the wall was wrong. Couldn't get any work done.

Reply

Sorry about that. The password on the wall was from before our router change and the connection dropped twice on Tuesday. I've replaced the sign and asked our provider to check the line. It's not much use calling it free Wi-Fi if it doesn't work. Thanks for your patience. Chris, manager

Why it works: Concedes the specific fault, with a touch of honesty.

Example · Card-only · café, Edinburgh

Review

No cash accepted and no sign. Had to leave coffee on the counter and walk to a cash machine. A hassle for a coffee.

Reply

I'm sorry, and thanks for saying. We're card-only to keep the till simple, and there was no sign on the door. I've put one up now and updated our profile. Next time you pass, the coffee's on us. Lucy, owner

Why it works: Accepts the missing sign, which was the real fault.

Example · No seats · café, Norwich

Review

Nowhere to sit at all at 11am on a Saturday. Staff said it was always like this at weekends. Could have told us on the website.

Reply

Thank you, that's fair. Saturday mornings are our busiest time and the seating is limited, so I've added a note to our listing and a few stools to the front window. Takeaway is quick, and weekdays are quieter if you'd like a table. Sorry we didn't make it clearer. Rob, owner

Why it works: Accepts that it was a communication gap and offers an alternative.

Oat milk and allergens

Allergen concerns are the one time to say very little in public. Never discuss ingredients, symptoms or causes in the reply and never suggest the writer is mistaken. The Food Standards Agency publishes allergen guidance for food businesses, and following it in your kitchen matters more than anything you write online.

Example · Oat milk charge · café, Bath

Review

Charged extra for oat milk and nothing on the menu said so. Not a big amount but it felt sneaky.

Reply

Thank you for raising this. We charge a little extra for plant milks because they cost us more, but it was not clear on the menu and that's our mistake. I've changed the board and the till screen so it's visible before you order. I'm sorry for the surprise. Please email me at [email] and I'll sort it out. Imran, owner

Why it works: Explains the reason briefly, admits the unclear menu and makes it visible.

Example · Allergen worry · café, Leeds

Review

I asked about nuts in a brownie and the staff member wasn't sure. I felt uneasy about eating there.

Reply

Thank you for telling us, and I'm sorry you didn't get a clear answer. Allergen questions need a confident, accurate response and I take this seriously. Please ring me on [phone] so I can find out what happened and make sure our team are trained properly. Natalie, owner

Why it works: Very short, serious and takes everything offline.

Cake, kind staff and dog-friendly praise

Example · Cake praise · tearoom, York

Review

The lemon drizzle is unreal. Light, zingy and the slice was massive. I went back the next day for another.

Reply

Thank you, that really made our morning. Our lemon drizzle is a recipe from Jean's grandmother and she'll be thrilled that it got a second visit. It's baked fresh each morning, so come early on Saturdays. Hope to see you again soon. Mel, owner

Why it works: Gives a detail only this café has, which is what makes it genuine.

Example · Friendly staff · café, Cardiff

Review

Zack remembered my name and my order on my second visit. Small thing, but it made me feel welcome.

Reply

What a lovely thing to say. I'll tell Zack today. Remembering a name and an order is exactly what we want the café to feel like. Thanks for coming back. Lena, owner

Why it works: Credits the person and the behaviour.

Example · Dog-friendly praise · café, Hastings

Review

Our spaniel got a bowl of water and a biscuit without us asking. The nicest dog-friendly café on this stretch of coast.

Reply

Thank you, and give your spaniel a pat from us. Dogs are always welcome on a lead, and the water bowl is by the door whenever you need it. We're happy you had a good visit. Jack, owner

Why it works: Short, warm and reinforces the policy.

Example · Loyalty card complaint · coffee shop, Liverpool

Review

Got my loyalty card stamped 9 times and was told the offer had changed and the card was no longer valid. Rubbish.

Reply

I'm sorry, and I understand why that was annoying. When we changed the scheme we should have honoured every card already in people's pockets, and that was our mistake. Please bring your card in and we'll give you the reward you earned. Thank you for flagging it. Anita, owner

Why it works: Concedes the mistake and makes it right in public without a fuss.

When to answer and when to ring

  • Reply once, calmly, in public. Then move to email or phone.
  • Fix the information. If the complaint is about something your profile should have said (hours, laptop rules, card-only), update the profile. Readers notice.
  • Don't name staff. "I'll speak to the team" is enough.
  • Check the facts. Till logs and the rota are better than memory.
  • If it looks fake, say so neutrally, ask for the date and visit, and report it. See fake Google reviews and removing Google reviews.

Frequently asked questions

How do I reply to a review saying my coffee was bad?

Thank them, say you're sorry it wasn't right, name one likely cause if you know it and offer to remake it. Don't argue about taste. A quick, humble reply reads well to everyone who sees it.

What should I say to a customer upset about laptop rules?

Give the reason in a sentence, own any unclear signage and say where the rule is now written. Telling people at the counter and putting it on your Google profile avoids repeat reviews.

Can I respond to a review about an oat milk surcharge?

Yes. Explain briefly why plant milks cost more, admit if the menu wasn't clear and say you've fixed it. Don't quote prices in the reply. Show them on the board and till instead.

How should a café reply to an allergen complaint?

Keep it short and take it offline: apologise, say you take it seriously and give a phone number or email. Don't discuss ingredients, symptoms or causes in public. Follow the Food Standards Agency's guidance in the kitchen.

Is it OK to say we're card-only in a reply?

Yes, plainly and politely, along with an apology if there was no sign. Add the information to your profile and put a sign on the door so the next customer knows.

Should I answer star-only reviews?

Yes. A short, warm thank-you takes seconds and shows readers you are paying attention. For a low rating with no comment, thank them and invite them to say what went wrong.
