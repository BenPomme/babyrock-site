Review response examples for dental practices

Twelve replies to the reviews dental practices get, written so they never confirm a patient or mention treatment: waits, NHS and private confusion, reception, emergencies and praise.

The short answer

A safe dental review reply thanks the reviewer, speaks about your practice in general terms, never confirms that the person is a patient, and never mentions treatment, appointments or any health detail. Invite them to contact the practice manager by phone or email instead. These examples show how to stay warm without breaching confidentiality.

The rule that changes everything: don't confirm they're a patient

In most trades, the strongest reply to a complaint mentions the specific problem. In dentistry, that instinct can cause harm. If a reviewer writes about their treatment and you reply about the same treatment, you have confirmed in public that they are your patient and discussed their care. The reviewer may have chosen to share that, but you have not been given permission to.

The General Dental Council publishes the Standards for the Dental Team, the standards that apply to everyone on the register. They are built around principles that include protecting patients' information, so before you reply to anything clinical, read the standards for yourself and check with your indemnity provider or practice compliance lead if you are unsure. This page is not legal or regulatory advice.

A safe public reply therefore follows four rules:

  • Never confirm the reviewer is a patient. Say 'thank you for your feedback' rather than 'thank you for visiting' or 'we're sorry about your appointment'.
  • Never mention treatment, diagnosis, medication, x-rays, fees paid or what was or wasn't done. Not even to defend yourself, and not even if the reviewer has already described it.
  • Keep it general. Talk about how the practice works and what you want to happen next, not about this person's care.
  • Move to a private channel. Give a phone number or email for the practice manager and say you will look into it.

Google's own page on replying to reviews says replies are public and appear under your business name. That alone is a reason to treat each reply as something anyone can read.

A tone guide for dental practices

Instead ofWriteBecause
“We're sorry your treatment went badly”“We're sorry to read this and would like to talk”The first confirms a patient and a treatment. The second doesn't.
“As we explained at your appointment”“We'd like to understand your experience”Referring to the appointment confirms they were there.
“Our records show you missed your check-up”“Please contact the practice manager so we can look into this”Records are private. Don't quote them.
“All our clinicians are fully qualified”“We take every concern seriously”Credentials aren't an answer to a complaint, and general claims can sound defensive.
“Dr Patel did nothing wrong”“We'll look into this fully”Never defend or name individual clinicians in public.
“We're sorry you feel that way”“We're sorry to hear this”Dismissing a feeling doubles the complaint.

Sign with a role, not a clinician's name: 'Practice manager' or 'The team at [practice name]'. A named person is easier to approach but harder to protect.

Waiting times and running late

Waiting is the most common non-clinical complaint, and the easiest to answer safely because it doesn't need any health information.

Example · Waiting room delays · dental practice, Leeds

Review

Sat in the waiting room for 45 minutes and nobody said a word. Reception was too busy to even look up. Disappointing.

Reply

Thank you for taking the time to write this. We're sorry that you were left waiting without any explanation, because our team should be updating anyone who is waiting. We'd like to hear more, so please contact our practice manager on [phone] or at [email]. The team

Why it works: Apologises for the communication, which is a general process, and doesn't refer to an appointment, a clinician or a treatment.

Example · Always running late · practice, Bristol

Review

Every time I come here the appointments run late. I've stopped bothering with the 'thank you for your patience' signs.

Reply

Thank you for your honest feedback. A practice should be realistic about timing and tell people clearly when things are running late. We're looking at how we do this and would welcome your suggestions. Please call [phone] or email [email]. Practice manager

Why it works: Takes a general lesson from the complaint without confirming individual visits.

NHS or private: cost confusion

Many practices offer both NHS and private care, and clients often don't understand the difference until the bill arrives. The NHS website has a general page on NHS dental services with links on charges and how to find an NHS dentist. In a reply, don't discuss what anyone paid or what they were or weren't offered. Don't mention fees or amounts, and don't say which option anyone chose.

Example · Cost confusion · practice, Manchester

Review

Wasn't clear what was NHS and what was private. Felt misled when I got the bill. Poor communication.

Reply

Thank you for telling us. Being clear about what is NHS and what is private matters to us, and we're sorry if any information was not as clear as it should be. Please contact our practice manager on [phone] or at [email] so we can look into your concern properly. The team at [practice name]

Why it works: Acknowledges the general principle (clarity) without discussing the bill, the treatment or the patient.

Example · NHS places · practice, Cardiff

Review

Called to join as an NHS patient and was told you aren't taking any. Why list yourselves as an NHS practice then?

Reply

Thank you for your message. NHS availability can change and we know it's frustrating when we can't offer a place. For up-to-date options, the NHS 'find a dentist' service is the place to check. If you have a question about how our practice is listed, please email [email]. Practice manager

Why it works: Answers in general terms and points to the NHS service. Doesn't say anything about a specific person.

Reception, booking and urgent appointments

Example · Rude reception · practice, Glasgow

Review

Phoned for an appointment and the receptionist was dismissive. Hung up feeling like a nuisance.

Reply

Thank you for letting us know. Everyone who calls our practice should be treated politely, and we're sorry that wasn't your experience. We'd like to look into this properly, so please contact our practice manager on [phone] or at [email]. The team

Why it works: Doesn't confirm they're a patient: 'everyone who calls' covers any caller.

Example · Couldn't get an emergency slot · practice, Sheffield

Review

Rang in a lot of discomfort and was told there was nothing available for days. If you can't help people in need, don't call yourself a practice.

Reply

We're sorry to read this. We know that people want help quickly when something is wrong, and we'd like to understand what happened with the call. Please contact our practice manager on [phone] or [email]. If you need urgent help now, NHS 111 can direct you to local services. The team

Why it works: Safe pointer to a signposting service, no health detail, no clinical judgement. Note that you shouldn't comment on what was or wasn't clinically needed.

Example · Fitted in quickly · practice, Nottingham

Review

Rang in the morning in a bit of a state and they found a space that same day. Everyone was kind. Can't thank them enough.

Reply

Thank you for taking the time to write this. We always try to help people who need us quickly, and it's lovely to know the team made a difference. We wish you well. The team at [practice name]

Why it works: Warm but doesn't confirm a patient or an appointment. Don't describe what the visit involved even if the reviewer did.

Praise, anxious patients and children

Positive reviews carry the same rule. Even a lovely review can lead to an over-friendly reply that confirms the person is a patient and mentions their care. Thank them for the kind words, speak about the team's values, and stop there.

Example · Anxious patient praise · practice, Bath

Review

I've been terrified of dentists my whole life and the whole team here were so patient and calm. First time I haven't dreaded it. Thank you.

Reply

Thank you so much for these kind words. Helping people feel at ease is something the whole team cares about, and we're delighted to hear it made a difference. Thanks for taking the time to write. The team at [practice name]

Why it works: Doesn't say 'your visit', 'your treatment' or 'you did so well'. Talks about the team's approach instead.

Example · Children praise · family practice, Norwich

Review

Took our daughter and she actually asked when she could go back. They made it feel like a game. Brilliant with kids.

Reply

Thank you, that's lovely to read. We want children to feel relaxed about the dentist, and we're so pleased the team succeeded. Thanks for sharing this with other families. The team at [practice name]

Why it works: Doesn't confirm the child or the visit, doesn't name the child or the age, and focuses on the team's approach.

Example · Concern about a child's visit · practice, Edinburgh

Review

My son came out upset and wouldn't say what happened. I'm not impressed with how he was handled.

Reply

Thank you for raising this. We take any concern about a child's experience very seriously and want to look into it properly. Please contact our practice manager directly on [phone] or [email] so that we can listen and respond privately. The team

Why it works: Serious but short. Doesn't mention the child, any treatment or any detail; moves to a private conversation.

Reviews that cross a line

A reviewer who describes treatment in detail, names a clinician, alleges negligence, or says a procedure caused harm has made a complaint that belongs in your complaints process, not in a public thread. Reply once with a neutral, general message and the practice manager's contact details. Don't argue the clinical facts. If there's a formal route for complaints, say that you'd like them to use it. Your indemnity provider can advise you on this.

If a review looks fake, from a competitor or from someone who was never a patient, you still must not confirm or deny it. Say you cannot find any record of this feedback only if your own procedures say that is appropriate, otherwise reply neutrally and report it to Google. Frequently asked questions

Can I confirm that a reviewer is a patient in my reply?

No. Don't confirm or deny it, even if the reviewer says they are. The reply is public, so avoid words like 'your visit', 'your appointment' or 'your treatment', and invite them to contact the practice manager privately.

Can I mention treatment if the reviewer already did?

No. The reviewer's choice to share doesn't give you permission to discuss their care in public. Keep the reply general and move the conversation to a private channel. Check the GDC's standards and your indemnity provider if you are unsure.

How should I reply to a one-star review with no text?

Thank them for the feedback, say you'd like to understand their concern, and give the practice manager's phone or email. Don't assume it's from a patient and don't guess at the reason.

What should I say about NHS and private costs?

Speak generally about being clear on options and invite a private conversation. Don't mention fees, amounts or which option anyone chose. The NHS website has general information for patients.

Should I name the dentist in a reply?

Avoid it, especially in a response to a complaint. Sign with a role such as practice manager or with the practice name, and say the team will look into it.

What if the review is fake or from a competitor?

Don't confirm or deny that the person was ever a patient. Reply neutrally if you reply at all, and report the review to Google using the review reporting tool.

Is it wrong to reply to a positive review?

No, but keep it general. Thank the reviewer and describe the team's approach without saying 'we're glad your treatment went well' or similar.

Can I use these replies word for word?

Use them as a starting point. Swap in your own practice name, contact details and tone, and have your practice manager check the final wording against your own confidentiality policy.
