Review response examples for pubs and bars

Fourteen replies for the reviews landlords and bar managers actually get: slow service, a poor pint, being refused, music noise, dogs, toilets and quiz-night praise.

Updated 25 November 2026 · By the BabyRock team

The short answer

A good pub review reply thanks the customer, names the specific thing they raised (the pint, the wait, the door, the noise), owns what was yours and calmly states what wasn't, and ends with a way to talk offline. Keep it to three or four sentences and never accuse a customer of being drunk. These 14 examples show how.

Why pub and bar reviews need their own approach

A pub review is rarely about one thing. A one-star complaint about a Saturday night is usually the sum of a long bar queue, a loud group at the next table and a bill that felt too high. Readers know this, and they read your reply as a picture of how the place is run when it is busy and something goes wrong.

  • Most reviews are written the morning after. The writer is tired, sometimes hungover, and often remembers the mood better than the facts. Stay calm and factual.
  • Other customers were there. A regular who saw the whole evening will read your reply and judge whether it is fair.
  • Some complaints involve the law. Refusing service to someone who is drunk, or to someone without ID, is not rudeness. It is a legal duty, and the reply should say so without sounding smug.
  • Noise, dogs and queues split opinion. What one customer calls lively, another calls too loud. A reply that respects both usually works.

Google's guidance on replying to reviews is brief: replies are public, shown under your business name, and must follow its content policies. The judgement about tone is yours, and the examples below show one way to do it.

Bar service and queues

Waiting at the bar is the most common complaint in any pub. The best replies say what actually happened, which is usually staffing, a big order ahead of them or a till or card machine fault.

Example · Slow bar service on a busy night · city-centre pub, Nottingham

Review

Stood at the bar for 20 minutes on Friday and watched two people get served who arrived after me. Three staff, but one kept disappearing to the cellar. Not good enough.

Reply

Thanks for telling us, and I'm sorry about the wait. Friday was our busiest night since the football and the cellar needed a delivery moved, which pulled one of the team away. That's a rota problem and it's mine to fix. I've added a second person to the bar on Fridays from 7pm. If you spot me in there, come and say hello. Jo, manager

Why it works: The reply names the actual cause, takes ownership of the rota and states a specific change.

Example · Ignored at the bar · bar and grill, Leeds

Review

Waited ages and the barman kept serving the regulars first. Felt like we were invisible.

Reply

I'm sorry, that's not how we want anyone to feel. Serving order should be who was there first, regardless of whether we know you. I've talked to the team about it this week and asked them to acknowledge people with a nod when they're waiting. Please message me at [email] if you'd like to tell me more. Owen, bar manager

Why it works: Acknowledges the feeling without calling the customer wrong, and gives a small, believable change.

Example · Card machine down · pub, Derby

Review

Machine wasn't working, no sign up, had to walk to a cash point mid-round. Cash would have been fine if we'd known.

Reply

Fair point, thank you. Our card terminal dropped out for an hour on Saturday and I should have put a sign on the bar straight away. I now keep a laminated card at the till for exactly this. Sorry for the walk, and I hope your next round goes through quicker. Tasha, landlady

Why it works: Admits the missing sign, which was the actual fault, and describes the fix.

The pint itself

Cellar quality is a topic where customers can be right, and a quick, humble reply earns more than a defence. Don't promise what you can't check. Say you'll look at the lines, the barrel or the glassware, and mean it.

Example · Flat lager · pub, Stoke-on-Trent

Review

Pint of lager was flat and warm. Sent it back and the second one wasn't much better. A shame because the place is nice.

Reply

Thank you for coming back with the second pint, and sorry neither was right. Flat and warm usually points to a gas or line problem, and I've asked our cellar service to come out this week. It's not something we want to hear twice. If you pop in again, tell the bar it's on me. Marcus, landlord

Why it works: Specific about the likely cause, doesn't promise a refund in public, and invites them back.

Example · Cloudy cask ale · real ale pub, Harrogate

Review

Ordered a pint of the guest bitter. Looked cloudy and tasted off. Barman said it was meant to look like that.

Reply

Thanks for raising this. A cask beer that's still settling can look hazy, but it should never taste off, and I'm sorry that was how it was explained. I've taken that barrel off and asked the team to offer a taste first whenever someone queries a pint. Please come in and ask for me. Ian, licensee

Why it works: Doesn't contradict the barman rudely, but fixes the process and takes the beer off sale.

Refusals, ID checks and door policy

This is where replies can go wrong. Under the Licensing Act 2003 it is an offence for staff to knowingly sell alcohol to someone who is drunk, and selling to under-18s is also covered by the same Act. You don't need to quote law in a reply. You do need to stay factual, never describe the customer's state, and never say they were rude. This page is not legal advice.

Example · Refused service · town-centre bar, Swansea

Review

Refused a drink at 11pm for no reason. Bar staff were rude and wouldn't explain. Never going back.

Reply

Thank you for letting us know. I can't discuss individual customers in public, but I can say we all have a duty to refuse service when we think someone has had enough, and I'm sorry if it wasn't put kindly. I've reminded the team to explain this politely. If you'd like to talk it through, please message me. Chris, general manager

Why it works: Stays factual, doesn't accuse or describe the customer, and still apologises for tone.

Example · ID check · pub, Exeter

Review

We're all 30 and they wanted passports from the lot of us. Ridiculous. Felt like a power trip.

Reply

I understand the frustration after a long wait. We ask for ID from anyone who looks under 25, and with a full table the check can feel heavy-handed. We accept a photo driving licence, passport or an accredited PASS card, and we make no exceptions. Thank you for your patience, and sorry it spoiled the start of your evening. Faye, bar manager

Why it works: Explains the rule in plain words without lecturing, and acknowledges the annoyance.

Example · Door policy · late bar, Newcastle

Review

Turned away at the door, with no reason. Group of six lads. The bouncer wouldn't say why.

Reply

Thanks for the review, and I'm sorry the door staff didn't explain. Our door policy covers group size, capacity and dress, and entry is always at the door team's discretion. We try to be clear and I'll ask them to explain the reason every time. Message me if you'd like to talk it over. Dean, venue manager

Why it works: Doesn't pretend the door was wrong, but fixes the missing explanation.

Quiz nights, music and the big match

Events create the loudest praise and the loudest complaints. Name the event in the reply, because readers searching for "pub quiz near me" will see it.

Example · Quiz night praise · village pub, Cotswolds

Review

Tuesday quiz is the highlight of our week. Questions were fun, Dave the quizmaster is a legend and the cheese toastie round was inspired.

Reply

Thank you, that's lovely. Dave will be insufferable now. The toastie round was Sue's idea, so I'll pass it on to the kitchen. Quiz starts at 8 every Tuesday, with the last question done by 10. Come with a team, and bring your own pen. Gareth, landlord

Why it works: Names the person and the round, gives the time details a new reader needs.

Example · Live music too loud · pub with a stage, York

Review

Went for a quiet drink and the band was so loud we couldn't talk. Had to leave. Should have said on Google that there was live music.

Reply

Thank you, and I'm sorry the evening didn't go as hoped. We have live music on Friday and Saturday evenings, and I've now added it to our listing so people know. I'll also ask the band to keep it down before 9pm so there's still room for a chat. Anna, landlady

Why it works: Fixes the information gap and offers a reasonable compromise, without blaming the customer.

Example · Match not shown · sports bar, Glasgow

Review

Told you were showing the rugby, got there and it was the football on every screen. Not what was advertised.

Reply

I'm sorry about the mix-up. We do show the rugby, but two live games clashed on Saturday and the football got the main screens. I should have said so on our page. I've started posting the screens plan each Friday, so you'll know what's on before you come. Craig, manager

Why it works: Admits the missing information and puts a specific habit in place.

Beer garden, dogs and toilets

Example · Beer garden · riverside pub, Oxford

Review

Lovely spot, but the beer garden was soaked and half the tables were out of action. Would've been good to know before we made the trip.

Reply

Thank you, and sorry about the soggy table. After heavy rain we move the garden tables to dry ground, but I should have updated our page that day. I'll post when the garden is closed from now on, and the inside bar is always open if the weather turns. Hope to see you in drier weather. Mike, landlord

Why it works: Treats the weather as something to communicate, not defend.

Example · Dog complaint · country pub, Lake District

Review

A dog was on the sofa next to our table and the staff did nothing. Not hygienic.

Reply

Thank you for letting us know. We welcome dogs in the bar area, but they shouldn't be on the furniture, and the team should have asked the owner to move it. I've reminded them about that. Assistance dogs are always welcome anywhere in the pub, as the law requires. Abbie, landlady

Why it works: Balances a dog-friendly policy with a fair rule. The assistance-dog line is based on the [Equality Act 2010](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/contents), which sets duties on service providers.

Example · Toilets · pub, Reading

Review

Great pub, but the toilets were filthy on Saturday night. Paper on the floor and no soap. Let down by that.

Reply

Thank you, and you're right to say it. The toilets should be checked every hour on a busy night, and that didn't happen on Saturday. We've added a tick-sheet on the door and I'm sorry for the soap. Please say hello next time and I'll show you the new routine. Pete, landlord

Why it works: Concedes the point and describes a visible, checkable fix.

Praise and star-only ratings

Example · Staff praise · neighbourhood pub, Brighton

Review

Rosie behind the bar remembered our order from last month and recommended a stout we'd never have tried. That's what a local should feel like.

Reply

Thank you so much. I'll pass this to Rosie before tonight's shift, and I'd bet she's already planning the next recommendation. Spotting a regular's glass is a skill. See you next time. Tom, landlord

Why it works: Credits a person and the behaviour, without gushing.

Example · Star rating only · local, Cardiff

Review

(5 stars, no comment)

Reply

Thank you for the five stars. Good to know you enjoyed it. See you at the bar. The team at the Crown

Why it works: Short, warm and in the pub's voice.

When to answer and when to ring

  • Reply once, calmly, in public. Then deal with the person privately if they respond.
  • Never describe a customer's state. Drunk, aggressive or rude are your view, not facts, and they make you look worse.
  • Don't name or blame staff. Say you'll speak to the team.
  • Check your own records. CCTV, till logs and the incident book should come first, not memory.
  • If a review looks fake or abusive, say so neutrally, ask for details and report it. See fake Google reviews and removing Google reviews.

Frequently asked questions

How should a pub reply to a review about being refused service?

Stay factual and short. Say you can't discuss individual customers publicly, that staff have a duty to refuse service when someone has had enough, and invite them to message you. Never describe the person as drunk or rude. Sales of alcohol are covered by the Licensing Act 2003.

Should I reply to a review about a pint of bad beer?

Yes, quickly and without defending the beer. Say you'll check the lines or barrel, thank them for telling you, and invite them back. A fast, humble answer reads better to other drinkers than a perfect pint ever could.

Can I mention ID rules in a reply?

Yes, in plain words. Say you ask for ID from anyone who looks under 25 or whatever your policy is, that you accept only valid photo ID, and that you're sorry it slowed things down. Don't mention the customer's age or appearance.

How do I answer a complaint about live music being too loud?

Thank them, say when live music is on and when it finishes, and say you'll look at the volume. Add the music nights to your Google listing so the next customer knows before they arrive.

What do I say to a star-only review in a pub?

Keep it short and warm: thank them for the rating, say you hope to see them again and sign with the pub's name. For a low star-only rating with no comment, thank them and invite them to say what went wrong.

How many of my pub's reviews should I answer?

Aim for all of them, starting with complaints and anything involving safety, refusals or staff. Short thank-you replies to five-star reviews take seconds and show other customers the pub is paying attention.
