Review response examples for restaurants, pubs and cafés

Fifteen realistic replies for the reviews hospitality owners get: slow service, cold food, lost bookings, allergens, delivery and praise.

Updated 25 November 2026 · By the BabyRock team

The short answer

A good restaurant review reply thanks the guest, names the specific problem or dish, owns what went wrong without excuses, and says what you will do next. Keep it to three or four sentences, sign with your first name and role, and take allergen, illness and refund conversations offline. These 15 examples show how.

Why hospitality replies are different

A restaurant review is often written the same evening, on a phone, by someone who was hungry, tired or celebrating. Replies are read by the next table deciding where to book. That makes a few things matter more than they do for a plumber or a dentist.

  • Timing is part of the story. A Saturday-night complaint about a wait is rarely about one dish. It's about how the room was run, so the reply should say something about the room.
  • Everyone can check you. Menus, booking systems and rotas are all records. A reply that says "we never take bookings" when you do will be read by someone who knows better.
  • Third parties are involved. Delivery apps, booking platforms and hired drivers all affect the experience. You can explain, but customers still blame the name above the door.
  • Food safety is serious. Allergies and illness are the one area where a friendly reply can do harm. Take them offline and don't offer advice.

Google's own guidance on replying to reviews is short. It says replies are public, are shown under your business name and are checked against content policies. The rest is judgement, which the examples below try to show.

Two other habits help. Sign with your first name and role ("Dani, owner" or "Sam, kitchen manager"), because hospitality customers like knowing who runs the place. And keep it to three or four sentences. A long defence under a short complaint makes the complaint look bigger.

Waiting, service and booking problems

These are the most common one- and two-star complaints in restaurants. They are also the easiest to answer well, because the fix is usually something you can state.

Example · Long wait for mains · gastropub, Bristol

Review

Booked for 7.30. Starters came quickly, then 55 minutes for the mains. No one explained. Food was lovely but we left feeling ignored.

Reply

I'm sorry, and thank you for being fair about the food. A 55-minute gap between courses is too long, and nobody coming to tell you was a second mistake. The kitchen had a large table ahead of you and we didn't say so. I've asked the floor to update tables if a course runs over 20 minutes. If you email me at [address], I'd like to make this up to you. Helen, owner

Why it works: The reply names the cause (a large table ahead), the missing communication and a rule change.

Example · Booking lost · Italian restaurant, Edinburgh

Review

Booked online for four at 8pm for a birthday. Turned up and they had no record. Forty minutes at the bar.

Reply

I'm so sorry. Looking at the system, your booking went through our online form but didn't reach the book on Thursday, and that's our fault, not yours. A birthday evening shouldn't start with forty minutes at the bar. Please email me at [address] and I'll arrange something for your next visit. Marco

Why it works: Specific about how it was lost, no blame on the guest, no refund promised in public.

Example · Table given away · bistro, Manchester

Review

We were 15 minutes late (called ahead) and our table had gone. Rude response when we asked why.

Reply

Thank you for telling us, and sorry about the way we handled it. We hold tables for 15 minutes and your call should have changed that. I'm sorry nobody noted it and sorry you were met with an unfriendly reply. Please email me and I'll personally book you in. Anya, manager

Why it works: Admits a failure on the phone call, reinforces the 15-minute policy without hiding behind it.

Food quality, portions and value

Example · Cold food · neighbourhood café, Cardiff

Review

Lukewarm eggs benedict, and the hollandaise had split. Poor for the price.

Reply

Thanks for telling us. Eggs benedict should arrive hot with a smooth sauce, and yours didn't. Our hollandaise is made fresh in small batches and I've asked the kitchen to check it before it goes out. I'm sorry we didn't get this right on your visit. Please email me at [address] and I will put it right. Dev, owner

Why it works: The reply focuses on the specific dish and makes a small, believable promise. Only promise what you will do.

Example · Sunday roast portion · pub, Norwich

Review

Sunday roast was tiny compared to the pictures online. Two slices of beef and a spoonful of veg. Not worth it.

Reply

Thank you, and I'm sorry the plate didn't match the picture. Our standard roast is three slices of beef with a full bowl of vegetables on the side, so something went wrong in the kitchen that day. I'd like to find out what, so please drop me a message with the date. Ray, landlord

Why it works: States what a normal plate should be, treats the review as a possible one-off, asks for a date.

Example · Service charge · modern British, London

Review

They added a discretionary service charge and then the card machine suggested a tip as well. Felt like a double charge.

Reply

Thanks for raising this, and I can see how it looked. The service charge on your bill was discretionary, and the card machine prompt should have been switched off for tables that already had it. I've asked the team to make this clear when the bill arrives. If you'd like the service charge adjusted, please email me. Imogen, general manager

Why it works: Acknowledges the confusion, explains the discretionary charge, fixes the card machine prompt.

Allergens and health: take it offline

This is the one category where the public reply should be minimal. Never discuss ingredients, symptoms or causes in the reply, never suggest the reviewer is mistaken, and never give medical advice. The Food Standards Agency has allergen guidance for food businesses which you should follow in your kitchen. A review reply is not the place to discuss it.

Example · Allergen concern · family restaurant, Leeds

Review

Told the waiter my son has a nut allergy and still got a dish with a pesto sauce on the side. We spotted it before eating. Terrifying.

Reply

I'm very sorry, and thank you for catching it. This is a serious matter and I want to look into it properly with you. Please ring me on [phone] or email [address] so we can talk through what happened. Jo, owner

Why it works: Doesn't explain or defend in public, doesn't mention ingredients, offers a direct line.

Takeaway and delivery

Customers often blame you for a late or cold delivery even when a driver on an app handled the last leg. Say what you control, say honestly what you don't, and offer to help.

Example · Late delivery · takeaway, Glasgow

Review

Ordered through the app at 7pm, food arrived at 9. Chips were soggy and the curry cold.

Reply

Sorry, and thank you for telling us. Our kitchen had your order ready at 7.25, which we can see from our records, but the delivery from there took far longer than expected. Soggy chips are what a long journey does, and we shouldn't have let it go out like that. Please message us your order number and we'll raise it with the app. Priya, owner

Why it works: Honest about where the delay was, doesn't blame the customer or the driver by name.

Example · Missing items · pizza shop, Sheffield

Review

Paid for a garlic bread and two sauces and none of it arrived.

Reply

Sorry about that. Garlic bread and sauces should be in the bag every time. We're now ticking items off at the pass before sealing the bag. Message us with your order number and we'll sort a refund through the app. Sal

Why it works: Gives a practical process change and a route to a refund that doesn't happen in public.

Atmosphere, noise and dogs

Example · Noise · wine bar, Brighton

Review

Lovely wine list but we couldn't hear each other. Music and hard surfaces. Not for a quiet dinner.

Reply

Thanks for the honest note, and I'm glad you liked the wine list. You're right: the room is loud, especially on Friday and Saturday. We've started turning music down at 8pm and we're adding sound panels this winter. Sunday to Wednesday evenings are much quieter if that suits you better. Fran, owner

Why it works: Agrees with the complaint, names an actual fix and gives them a quieter slot.

Example · Dog-friendly praise · coastal pub, Whitby

Review

Brilliant dog-friendly pub. The staff brought a bowl of water without us asking and our spaniel got a sausage.

Reply

Thank you. A bowl of water is the minimum around here and the spaniel is welcome back any day. The sausage was Kit's idea. Hope the walk along the front was good too. Gareth, landlord

Why it works: Mentions a detail only that venue and visit could produce.

Example · Dogs not welcome complaint · café, Bath

Review

Sat outside with my assistance dog and was told to move. Disgusting.

Reply

I'm very sorry. Assistance dogs are welcome in every part of our café and I'm sorry that wasn't made clear to you. I've reminded everyone on the team. I'd like to hear exactly what was said, so please email me at [address]. Tara, owner

Why it works: Responds firmly to something that can cross into legal territory, without arguing or quoting law.

Praise, staff shout-outs and celebrations

Example · Staff shout-out · Indian restaurant, Leicester

Review

Our waiter Sunil talked us through the whole menu and remembered we didn't like spice. Best service we've had in ages.

Reply

Thank you so much. I'll read this out at the pass before tonight's service, and Sunil will be delighted. Remembering what a guest told him in the first five minutes is the whole job. See you next time. Amir, owner

Why it works: Credits the person, mentions the exact behaviour, says what you'll do with the praise.

Example · Birthday · tapas bar, Glasgow

Review

Brought my partner here for their 30th. The kitchen sent out a little dessert with a candle. It made the night.

Reply

Happy birthday to your partner. We love a surprise candle, and the team had great fun with it. Thanks for choosing us for the night. Come back for your next anniversary. Nuria

Why it works: Names the occasion and the small gesture without gushing.

Example · Star rating only · fish and chip shop, Hull

Review

(5 stars, no comment)

Reply

Thank you for the five stars. Good to know you enjoyed it. Cheers. The Harbour Fry team

Why it works: Brief, warm and in the shop's voice.

When to answer and when to ring

  • Reply publicly first, then move on. One calm reply shows every reader how you handle complaints. Then deal with the guest privately.
  • Never reveal what's on their booking. Don't mention party size, names, dates of private events or what they ordered unless they did first.
  • Don't offer free meals in public. Offer to talk, and decide the gesture in private.
  • Check your own records first. A reply that corrects the guest should come from the booking system or till, not from memory.
  • If a review looks fake, say so neutrally, ask for details, and report it. See fake Google reviews and removing Google reviews.

Frequently asked questions

Should I reply to every restaurant review?

Replying to all of them is a good aim, including short five-star ones. Prioritise complaints and anything involving health, safety or staff, since those are the reviews other diners read most carefully.

How do I reply to an allergen complaint?

Keep it short and take it offline: apologise, say you take it seriously and give a phone number or email. Don't discuss ingredients, symptoms or causes in public, and don't give medical advice. Follow the Food Standards Agency's guidance in the kitchen.

Who is responsible for a late delivery review?

Customers will blame you whatever the cause. Say what your kitchen did, be honest about what a delivery partner controls and offer to look into the order privately.

Can I mention the guest's booking or order in my reply?

Only if the reviewer already did. Don't reveal party size, names, dates or private events. Ask them to message you the details instead.

Should I offer a free meal in a public reply?

It's safer not to. Invite them to contact you and decide any gesture privately. A public offer can encourage copycat complaints.

What if the review is about an incident I can't find in my records?

Stay neutral. Say you can't find the visit, ask for the date and details, and invite them to contact you. Never accuse them of lying in public.

How quickly should I reply?

As soon as you can, ideally within a couple of days. Prompt replies show new diners you pay attention.

Can I use these replies word for word?

Use them as a guide. Swap in your own detail, dish and name, so your replies don't look identical.
