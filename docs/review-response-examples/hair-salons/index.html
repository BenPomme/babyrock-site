Review response examples for hair salons and beauty studios

Twelve realistic replies to the reviews salon owners get: colour that didn't turn out, a cut that's too short, running late, price surprises and good news.

Updated 25 November 2026 · By the BabyRock team

The short answer

A good salon review reply thanks the client, names the specific service, owns what went wrong without excuses, and offers a way to put it right in person, such as a consultation or a free adjustment. Keep it to three or four sentences, sign with your first name, and take skin reactions offline. These examples show how.

Why salon reviews are different

A hair review is about something the client wears on their head every day, and often about a date, a wedding or a job interview. People are rarely upset about 'a haircut'. They're upset that they feel different from how they hoped to feel. That shapes how a good reply sounds.

  • The result is visible and personal. A client who says the colour is wrong is looking at it in the mirror each morning. A reply that sounds defensive will be read as not caring about that.
  • There is usually a conversation you can have. Unlike a restaurant meal, hair can often be corrected. A reply that offers a quick consultation or a free adjustment, where you decide it's right, turns an angry review into a repair.
  • Expectations are set at the consultation. Many complaints come from a picture the client brought in and a result that was not achievable in one visit. You can say that kindly, but not as a way of blaming them.
  • Staff are the product. Clients book a person, not a salon. Praise for a stylist matters to that stylist, and criticism of one must never turn into naming them in public.
  • Skin and scalp reactions are a health matter. That's the one area where the public reply should be short and move offline.

Google's guidance on replying to reviews is brief: replies are public, appear under your business name, and are subject to content policies. Everything else is judgement, which the examples below try to show.

Colour, cut and result problems

These are the reviews that hurt most, because the client is unhappy with something they can see. Aim for specifics, a small believable promise and a route to a fix.

Example · Colour went wrong · salon, Leeds

Review

Went in for a caramel balayage and left with orange. Stylist said it would tone down after a wash. It hasn't. A lot of money spent on something I can't wear to work.

Reply

I'm sorry, and I can understand why you're upset. Orange tones after lightening are common on darker hair, but 'it'll settle' wasn't an answer, and a toner should have been discussed before you left. I'd like to see your hair in person and put it right. Please ring me on [phone] and I'll book you in with me for a colour correction consultation. Kelly, owner

Why it works: Gives an honest reason without hiding behind it, admits the missing step and offers a specific fix.

Example · Cut too short · barber and salon, Glasgow

Review

Asked for a trim and a 'little off the layers'. Came out about four inches shorter than I wanted. Never going back.

Reply

I'm sorry. A trim should be a trim, and four inches is far more than you asked for. I should have checked the length with you before the last cut, and I'm sorry we didn't. Hair does grow back, but I'd like to look at it with you and talk through the shape while it does. Please message us on [phone]. Alasdair, owner

Why it works: Takes the mistake on, doesn't argue about what was said, and offers to help with the in-between stage.

Example · Not what the picture showed · salon, Brighton

Review

Brought three photos of the colour I wanted. Result looked nothing like them. The stylist just said 'this is as light as we can go'.

Reply

Thank you for telling us, and I'm sorry you left disappointed. Some of the shades in those kinds of photos take more than one appointment to reach, and we should have told you that clearly at the start, not at the end. If you'd like, come in for a free half-hour chat about a plan for the next step. Please ring [phone]. Priya, salon manager

Why it works: Explains kindly that it may take several visits, admits the timing of the message was wrong and gives a next step.

Example · Great balayage · salon, Bristol

Review

Absolutely love my balayage. Jess listened to what I wanted, didn't push me into anything, and it grows out so softly. Booked my next one already.

Reply

Thank you, this made our morning. Soft grow-out is exactly what we aim for with balayage, and I'll tell Jess you said so. See you for the next one. Kelly

Why it works: Names the actual benefit (grow-out) so it's clearly not a template, and passes on the praise.

Time, bookings and the front desk

Running late, a booking that went missing or a short reply from reception rarely makes someone change salon on its own, but it appears in reviews all the time. Replies here should be calm and name what changes.

Example · Late appointment · hair salon, Manchester

Review

Appointment at 2. Sat there until 2.50 with no apology and nobody offering a drink. Not a great start.

Reply

You're right, and I'm sorry. Fifty minutes is too long to sit without anyone updating you, and an apology was the least you should have had. We had a colour that over-ran and we didn't tell you. From now on the front desk will let anyone waiting more than ten minutes know why. Please ring me on [phone] if you'd like to talk. Sam, owner

Why it works: Specific about the cause and the new rule; no excuses about being busy.

Example · Rude receptionist · salon, Cardiff

Review

Phoned to move my appointment and the girl on reception was short and made me feel like a nuisance.

Reply

Thank you for telling us, and I'm sorry that call went the way it did. Moving an appointment is a normal request and nobody should be made to feel they're a nuisance for asking. I'm going to go over how we handle calls with the whole team this week. If you'd like to tell me more, please email [email]. Bethan, owner

Why it works: Doesn't name or blame the person, apologises for the tone, commits to a conversation with the team.

Example · Booking lost · beauty studio, Edinburgh

Review

Booked online a week ago for lashes and a brow tint. Arrived and no record. They fitted me in, but I wasted my lunch break.

Reply

I'm sorry. The online booking should have appeared in our diary and it didn't, which is our problem and not yours. Thank you for being patient while we squeezed you in. I've asked the team to check each morning that the bookings app and the diary match. If you'd like to talk about it, please email [email]. Mhairi, owner

Why it works: Takes responsibility for the system failure, thanks the client for flexibility and states the practical fix.

Example · No-show complaint · salon, Norwich

Review

Charged a cancellation fee for a no-show but I cancelled by text the night before! Terrible.

Reply

Thank you for raising this. I can't see a message on our phone from the night before, so I'd like to look at it with you. If you cancelled with notice, the fee shouldn't apply and I'll put it right. Please ring [phone] or email [email] with the number you texted from. Sophie, owner

Why it works: Doesn't accuse, doesn't give in blindly. Asks for the evidence and keeps the details private.

Price surprises and add-ons

Price is a common complaint in salons because the final bill depends on hair length, thickness and the time a colour takes. The strongest defence is a reply that explains how you quote without sounding like you're quoting a rule.

Skin reactions and patch tests: take it offline

Colour and some beauty treatments can cause a reaction in some people. A public reply is the wrong place to discuss it. Don't say whether you think the product caused it, don't describe symptoms, and don't give medical advice. Say you're concerned, ask them to contact you, and, where it applies, say you'll follow your own procedure. Your product manufacturer and your insurer will have guidance you should follow privately.

The Health and Safety Executive (HSE) publishes guidance for salons on dermatitis and skin checks for staff. It is aimed at your team's safety rather than client complaints, but it is worth reading if you handle colour products.

Example · Patch test complaint · salon, Birmingham

Review

Had a patch test two days before and everything was fine. After colouring my scalp was very sore. Nobody at the salon seemed bothered.

Reply

I'm very sorry to read this. We take it seriously and I want to look into it with you directly. Please ring me on [phone] or email [email]. Ravi, owner

Why it works: Short, serious and offline. No cause, no symptoms, no advice.

Example · Patch test praise · colour specialist, Nottingham

Review

Genuinely appreciated that they insisted on a patch test and rescheduled me, rather than just doing it. Felt safe, not rushed.

Reply

Thank you, that's kind of you to say. Patch tests matter to us, and we'd rather rebook than risk it. Hope you love the colour when we do it. Eve

Why it works: Reinforces a good habit without explaining medical details.

Star-only reviews and quick thanks

Many reviews have no text at all. A short, warm reply costs you ten seconds and shows the next reader you pay attention.

Example · Star rating only · barber, Coventry

Review

(5 stars, no comment)

Reply

Thanks for the five stars. See you in four weeks for the next trim. Dean and the team

Why it works: Mentions the usual rebooking rhythm for a barber; short and warm.

Example · One star, no comment · salon, Hull

Review

(1 star, no text)

Reply

Thank you for rating us. We're sorry you weren't happy and we'd like to understand why. Please ring [phone] or email [email] and we'll look into it. Lisa, owner

Why it works: Doesn't assume anything, doesn't argue, makes a real offer of contact.

When to reply, and when to ring

  • Reply once in public, then talk privately. One calm reply shows every reader how you treat complaints. A long thread of back-and-forth does not.
  • Check the diary before you reply. If a review says the stylist was late, you can look at what happened. A reply based on the booking system is more credible than one based on memory.
  • Don't reveal what was done. Avoid naming services, colour formulas or details of a patch test unless the client already did.
  • Offer a fix you can deliver. A free adjustment is fine, but only offer what you will do. Keep any figures and refunds out of the public reply.
  • If the review looks fake or from a non-client, reply neutrally, ask for details, and report it to Google. See fake Google reviews and removing Google reviews.

Frequently asked questions

Should I reply to every salon review?

Replying to all of them is a good aim, including short five-star ones. Put complaints first, along with anything about skin reactions or a named member of staff, because those are the reviews future clients read most carefully.

How do I reply to a review about a colour that went wrong?

Apologise for the result, name the shade or service, say what you'll do, such as a consultation or adjustment, and give a phone number or email. Don't blame the client's hair or their reference photo in public.

What should I say about a skin reaction in a public reply?

Keep it short: say you're concerned and ask them to contact you directly. Don't discuss the cause, the product, or symptoms, and don't give medical advice. Follow your product manufacturer's and insurer's guidance in private.

Can I name the stylist in my reply?

Name a stylist for praise, if they'd be happy with it. Never name staff when replying to a complaint. Say you'll talk to the team instead.

Should I mention the cancellation fee or policy in a reply?

You can mention your policy in a calm sentence, but lead with empathy and offer to check the details privately. Avoid copying a long policy into a public reply.

What if I can't find the client's booking?

Stay neutral. Say you can't find the visit, ask for the date and the name it was booked under, and invite them to contact you. Never accuse them of making it up.

Should I offer a free service in a public reply?

It's safer to invite them to contact you and decide any gesture in private. A public offer can encourage copycat complaints.

Can I use these replies word for word?

Use them as a guide. Swap in your own service, details and name so your replies don't look identical, and so they pass the test of sounding like you.
