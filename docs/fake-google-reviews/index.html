Fake Google reviews: how to spot, report and reply

How to tell a fake from a genuine complaint, what evidence to keep, how to report it and what to say in public.

Updated 25 November 2026 · By the BabyRock team

The short answer

A fake Google review isn't based on a real experience of your business, such as a competitor's attack or a bought review. Check your records for a matching customer, note the timing, report it to Google as fake engagement, then reply factually without accusing anyone. In the UK, fake reviews are also banned by law.

The steps

  1. Check your recordsSearch your bookings, orders, invoices and till records for the name, date and details. A review you can't match to anyone is a lead, not proof.
  2. Look at the patternNote when it was posted, what else the account has reviewed and whether other reviews arrived close together with similar wording.
  3. Report it to GoogleReport the review from your Business Profile and choose the reason that fits. Google judges it against its fake engagement and conflict of interest rules.
  4. Reply once, factuallySay you can't find a record, invite them to get in touch and don't accuse anyone. The reply is for the people reading it.
  5. Appeal if refusedIf Google finds no violation, you can use the one-time appeal in the Reviews Management Tool, with a clear policy reason.

What counts as a fake review

Not every unfair review is a fake. A customer who was genuinely annoyed, exaggerated or got a detail wrong still had an experience with you. A fake review is different: it isn't based on a real experience of your business. Google's Prohibited and restricted content policy describes fake engagement as content that is not based on a real experience or does not accurately represent the place in question. It also lists paid-for reviews, content posted from multiple accounts by or at the request of one person, and content posted using tools designed to imitate genuine activity.

That policy also bans content based on a conflict of interest, such as current or former employment, a contractual relationship, or being an industry competitor. And it prohibits unusual volumes or patterns of reviews that look like an attempt to manipulate a place's rating.

So there are really four kinds of problem review, and they need different handling:

How to spot a fake

No single sign proves a review is fake. You are looking for several signs that point the same way. Treat each one as a reason to look closer, not as a verdict.

Start with your own records

  • No customer record. The name, date and details don't match any booking, order, invoice, job sheet or card payment. Allow for people using a partner's name or a different spelling.
  • Details that don't fit. The review describes a dish you don't serve, a service you don't offer, a member of staff who doesn't exist or a day you were closed.
  • Details that fit someone else. The facts match a different business, a different branch or a competitor's product.

Look at the timing

  • A cluster. Several one-star reviews within a short window, especially with similar wording or similar punctuation.
  • A trigger. The review appears just after a dismissal, a dispute, a refused refund, a price change or a falling-out you know about.
  • A mismatch with your rhythm. You were closed, on holiday or fully booked on the date the review implies.

Look at the reviewer's profile

  • A profile that has reviewed only your business, or only businesses in your sector in your town.
  • A profile with no history that was created close to the date of the review.
  • Several profiles with similar names, phrasing or patterns that line up in time.

Think about conflicts of interest

  • Competitor signs. The review praises a named rival, redirects customers or appears alongside five-star reviews on a competitor's page.
  • Ex-employee signs. It mentions internal matters customers wouldn't see, such as rotas, pay, management disputes or kitchen practices, or uses a staff nickname.
  • Personal grudge. The writer is a former partner, a neighbour in a dispute or someone you have had a falling-out with.

Be careful with the last two. You may be right, but unless you have clear evidence you can't prove who wrote it, and Google will judge on what it can see. Stay with what you can show: no matching record, a pattern or an obvious conflict.

How to gather evidence without accusing anyone

The aim is a short file you could hand to Google, your insurer or an adviser, built from facts rather than suspicion. Do this before you reply, because a reply can change what the review looks like.

  • Screenshot the review and the reviewer's public profile with the date and time, and keep the link to the review. Google explains how to get a link to a customer's review.
  • Write down what you searched and the result: bookings, orders, card receipts, appointment books, delivery logs and CCTV where you legally keep it. 'No record under that name between these dates' is a fact.
  • List what doesn't fit, such as a menu item, a service or a staff member that doesn't exist.
  • Note the timeline: the date of the review, any dispute or event before it and any other reviews that arrived nearby.
  • Save related messages: emails, texts or social posts from the same person or about the same dispute.
  • Keep it private. Share it with Google, an adviser or the authorities, not on social media. Naming someone as the author of a review in public risks turning a bad review into a worse problem.

How to report a fake review to Google

You report from your Business Profile, and you must be a verified manager. As Google's Report inappropriate reviews page describes it:

  1. Go to your Business ProfileOn Google Search or Maps, signed in with the account that manages the business.
  2. Select Read reviewsFind the review you think is fake.
  3. Select ReportIt sits next to the review. Then pick the reason that best fits the problem.
  4. Send the reportGoogle says evaluation typically takes several days.

You can also use Google's Reviews Management Tool to report a new review and to follow what happens to it. In the tool, a flagged review shows as decision pending until it is evaluated. If Google reviews it and finds no violation, the status shows that, and you can submit a one-time appeal. You can select up to 10 reviews, fill out the form and you will get an email with the result. If the review violates the policy it is removed, and if it complies it stays up. Our guide to removing Google reviews walks through reporting, status and appeals step by step.

Choosing the reason

Pick the reason that matches the problem you can evidence. If you can't show a link to a real visit, the conflict-of-interest or not-based-on-a-real-experience reasons fit better than profanity. Don't choose a reason you can't support just to improve your chances. Google assesses the review against the policy, not against the box you tick.

A cluster of fakes

If you see a burst of reviews that looks like an attack, report each one separately and note that they arrived together. Google's policy specifically covers unusual volumes or patterns of reviews that suggest an attempt to manipulate a place's rating. If anyone contacts you offering to make them stop in return for money, that is the extortion case covered on Google's page for reporting negative review extortion scams. Don't pay.

What to say in a public reply

Reply once, even if you've reported it. The review may stay for days, and everyone who reads it will want to see what you said. The tone to aim for is calm, factual and open. You don't say the review is fake, because you can't prove it publicly, and you don't name or describe the person. You say what you can see and invite them to talk.

Example · 1 star · restaurant, Bristol, no matching booking

Review

Waited an hour for starters on Saturday night and the manager was rude to us. Avoid.

Reply

Thank you for your review. We want to look into this properly, but we can't find a booking or a record matching your visit on Saturday, and our kitchen log shows no delays on that service. If you can email us at [email address] with the name the table was booked under, we'll look into it and come back to you. Dev, owner

Why it works: It states what you checked, doesn't call anyone a liar and gives the writer an easy way to prove it was real. If it was a genuine mix-up, they can say so.

Example · 1 star · plumber, Leeds, wrong business

Review

Said they would be here at 9 and never turned up. Left us without heating for two days. Useless.

Reply

I'm sorry to read this, but I don't think this was our company. We haven't had a booking in your name and our diary shows no missed visits that week. You may be thinking of another firm with a similar name. If you'd like to check, please ring me on [phone] and I'll go through our records with you. Ellie Marsh, Marsh Heating

Why it works: It gives a gentle alternative explanation and offers to check, instead of attacking.

Example · 1 star · salon, Glasgow, burst of reviews

Review

Awful. Stay away from here. (Three similar reviews from new accounts arrived the same afternoon.)

Reply

Thank you for taking the time to write. We pride ourselves on looking after every client, so we'd like to understand what went wrong. We can't match this review to a visit, so please contact us at [email address] so we can look into it. Kirsty, owner

Why it works: It doesn't mention the cluster or accuse anyone. The reply stays short and leaves the pattern to Google, to whom you report it.

A reply template to adapt

'Thank you for your review. We take feedback seriously and want to look into this. We can't find a record of [booking / order / visit] matching the details you've described, so we'd be grateful if you could contact us at [email / phone] with more information. [First name], [role]'

What not to write

  • 'This review is fake' or 'you're a competitor'. If you're wrong, you have insulted a real customer. If you're right, you have no proof in public.
  • A named accusation, such as 'this is from our ex-employee Jordan'. That is personal information and a defamation risk, and Google's policy bans posting personal information without consent.
  • Threats of legal action. If you think the review is defamatory, take proper advice privately. We don't give legal advice.
  • Anything offered in exchange for removing or changing the review. Google's policy bans merchants from offering payment, discounts, free goods or services for revising or removing a negative review.

UK law on fake reviews

In the UK, fake reviews are a matter for consumer law as well as Google's rules. The Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 lists banned practices in Schedule 20. Paragraph 13 covers submitting, or commissioning another person to submit or write, a fake consumer review, or a consumer review that conceals the fact it has been incentivised. It also covers publishing consumer reviews or review information in a misleading way. The Act's contents page shows the full text, and the legislation site records that the paragraph came into force on 6 April 2025.

What this means in practice for you:

  • It covers writing and commissioning. Buying fake reviews, or asking someone else to write them, is within the ban. That includes paying for five-star reviews of your own business and posting negative reviews about a competitor.
  • It applies to you as well as to the people attacking you. Don't write, buy or arrange reviews yourself, and don't offer rewards for reviews without being open about it. The safe route is to ask genuine customers and let them say what they think.
  • The Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) can enforce these rules directly. Its guidance for businesses on reviews is collected on gov.uk: Reviews and social media endorsements: what businesses and content creators must know.

This page is general information, not legal advice. If you believe you are the victim of a coordinated campaign, a defamatory review or blackmail, speak to a solicitor, your trade body or the police where there is a threat or demand for money. Keep your evidence file ready.

If Google doesn't remove it

Sometimes the review stays even though you are sure it is fake. Google can only act on what it can assess against its policy, and a refusal doesn't mean the review is genuine. You still have good options:

  • Use the one-time appeal if you have a clear policy reason and can state it plainly, such as 'not based on a real experience and posted by a former employee' alongside the facts you can show.
  • Keep your calm public reply. It tells readers you checked and invited contact, which is often enough to neutralise a bad review.
  • Earn more genuine reviews. Ask real customers, at the right moment, with a link or QR code. Google's page on creating a link or QR code to request reviews shows how. Don't offer anything in return and don't ask only the people you expect to be positive.
  • Don't buy reviews to bury it. That is exactly what Google's policy and UK law prohibit.
  • Reply to everything. A profile where every review gets a considered answer makes one odd review stand out as odd.

Frequently asked questions

How can I tell if a Google review is fake?

Look for several signs together: no matching customer record, details that don't fit your business, a cluster of similar reviews in a short time, a reviewer profile with no history, or a link to a competitor or former employee. One sign alone proves nothing.

Will Google remove a review I report as fake?

Only if it breaks Google's content policy, for example fake engagement or a conflict of interest. Google's help says evaluation typically takes several days, and you can follow the status in the Reviews Management Tool. If it finds no violation you can use a one-time appeal.

Should I tell people in my reply that the review is fake?

No. You usually can't prove it in public, and if you're wrong you insult a real customer. Say you can't find a record, invite them to contact you and let Google judge the report.

Is it illegal to post fake reviews in the UK?

The Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 bans submitting or commissioning a fake consumer review, as set out in Schedule 20 on legislation.gov.uk. This page is general information, not legal advice, so speak to a solicitor about your own case.

Can I report a competitor for posting fake reviews?

You can report the reviews to Google, which prohibits content from industry competitors under its conflict of interest rules. Don't name anyone publicly without proof. For legal steps, take advice or look at the CMA's guidance on gov.uk.

What if someone offers to remove bad reviews for money?

Don't pay. Google has a page for reporting negative review extortion scams, and its policy bans offering payment in exchange for removal of a negative review. Keep the messages as evidence.

Should I post my own good reviews to balance it out?

No. Writing or buying reviews of your own business is a fake review under Google's policy and, in the UK, within the banned practices in the 2024 Act. Ask genuine customers instead, without offering anything in return.
