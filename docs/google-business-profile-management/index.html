What Google Business Profile management actually includes

Google renamed Google My Business to Business Profile, but plenty of owners still search for the old name, so the two terms mean the same listing here. It is the panel that controls how your business looks on Google Search and Google Maps. Managing it is not one job. It is six small ones that repeat.

Reviews

Customers can review you at any time, and you can reply to each review from your profile. Google's help page on reading and replying to reviews explains how, and notes that your reply is public. Management here means reading new reviews promptly, thanking happy customers with something specific, and answering unhappy ones calmly without arguing or sharing private details.

Posts and updates

You can publish posts on your profile, such as an offer, an event or news. Google's page on creating posts lists the post types and what is allowed. A post is a reason for someone looking at your profile to see that the business is active.

Photos and videos

Photos show what a customer will find when they arrive: the shopfront, the inside, the menu, the work. Google's guidance on adding photos and videos covers format and quality. Management means adding new, accurate pictures now and then and removing ones that are out of date.

Opening hours and holiday hours

Wrong hours cause the most avoidable bad experiences: a customer walks to a closed door and then writes about it. Google lets you edit your business hours and set special hours for bank holidays and other days. Our guide to holiday hours on Google shows how.

Contact details, categories and attributes

Phone number, website, address, your main category and the attributes that apply to you (for example, whether you offer outdoor seating or are wheelchair accessible) all help customers decide. Management means checking that they are still true after a move, a new phone line or a change in what you offer.

Accuracy and access

Google's guidelines for representing your business say your name, address and details must reflect the real business. Keeping the profile accurate also means keeping control of it: knowing who has access and removing people who no longer need it.

Google changes the features inside a Business Profile from time to time. If a section described here looks different on your screen, follow the current wording in your own profile and in Google's help pages.

A realistic routine for each week if you do it yourself

You do not need an hour a day. Most small businesses can keep a profile healthy in about twenty minutes spread across the week, if the habit is fixed. Use this as a checklist and adjust it to how busy you are.

  • Monday, five minutes: reviews. Open your profile and read any new reviews. Reply to the easy ones straight away.
  • Monday, five minutes: the hard ones. Leave any angry review for a calm moment, but not for more than a day or two. Draft a short factual reply, then re-read it as if you were the customer. Our guide to responding to Google reviews has the structure.
  • Wednesday, five minutes: hours and details. Check the week ahead for bank holidays, closures or staff holidays. If anything changes, update your hours now. See holiday hours.
  • Friday, five minutes: a photo or a post. Add one real photo from the week, such as a new dish, a finished job or a quiet shopfront shot, or publish one short post about something happening next week.
  • First of the month, ten minutes: audit. Search your own business name in an incognito window. Check the phone number, website, hours and photos shown. Look at the list of people with access and remove anyone who should not have it.
  • After a busy spell: ask happy customers for a review. Our review QR poster works for that; it is in Spanish for now.

The reason owners fall behind is rarely difficulty. It is that the routine depends on remembering, and a busy week breaks the chain. If that sounds familiar, the next section describes an alternative.

What to check before you hire anyone

Whether it is a freelancer, an agency or a tool, the first conversation should be about access, not marketing. Your Business Profile is an asset that customers find you through, and it is hard to recover if someone leaves with the keys.

Access level: add them as a manager, not an owner

Google's page on adding, changing or removing users describes the roles. Primary owners and owners have the most control; managers can edit and reply but cannot do everything an owner can. Add helpers as a manager and keep ownership in your own Google account. If the relationship ends, you remove them yourself; you never depend on their cooperation.

  • Never hand over your Google password. Use the invite feature in the profile's users settings, so access is tied to the person's own account and can be removed in a click.
  • Check who is already the owner. If an old agency or ex-employee is listed, remove them or ask for ownership to be transferred before you start.
  • Ask what needs your approval. Replies to negative reviews, posts that make claims or offers, and any change to your name, address or category are worth signing off.

Other questions to ask

  • How are reviews answered? Ask to see three real replies. If they could belong to any business, they will read as generic to your customers.
  • Can they show consistency? Ask how fast reviews get a reply and what happens when the person is away.
  • What do they do about review requests? Be wary of anyone who promises more reviews through incentives or fake ones. Google's policies restrict both, and the damage lands on your profile.
  • What do you keep if you leave? The profile, the photos you supplied and the review history are yours. Check the contract says so.
  • Is the profile accurate? Never let anyone change your business name to add keywords. Google's representation guidelines require the real name, and a keyword-stuffed name risks suspension.

Frequently asked questions

What is Google Business Profile management?

It is the regular work of keeping your Google listing accurate and active: replying to reviews, posting, adding photos, updating hours and holiday hours, and checking details such as phone number and category. It covers what customers see on Google Search and Maps.

Is Google My Business the same as Google Business Profile?

Yes. Google renamed Google My Business to Business Profile. Many people still search for the old name, but it is the same listing and managed from the same place.

How often should I update my Business Profile?

Check it every week for new reviews and upcoming closures, and add a photo or post when you have something real to show. Do a fuller check once a month: details, photos and who has access.

Should I hire an agency or manage it myself?

If you have a few reviews a week and enjoy it, doing it yourself works. If you have several locations or want creative support, an agency can make sense. If time is the problem and you want to keep control of difficult replies, a tool is another option.

What access should I give a freelancer or agency?

Add them as a manager through the profile's users settings, and keep ownership in your own Google account. Never share your password. Google's page on adding, changing or removing users explains the roles.

Can BabyRock reply to bad reviews without me?
