How to respond to Google reviews

Where to reply, what to write, and 12 example replies for the reviews every small business gets.

Updated 25 November 2026 · By the BabyRock team

The short answer

To respond to a Google review, sign in at business.google.com, choose Read reviews, select the reply icon beside the review, write your answer and select Reply. Your profile must be verified. Thank the reviewer, mention one specific detail and keep it short. Google checks each reply against its policies before it appears publicly.

The steps

  1. Sign inGo to business.google.com and open your Business Profile. It has to be verified before you can reply.
  2. Open Read reviewsSelect Read reviews to see every review, newest first.
  3. Select the reply iconFind the review you want to answer and select the Reply icon next to it.
  4. Write your replyType your answer in the box. Use the formula below if you are not sure where to start.
  5. Select ReplyGoogle checks the reply against its content policies. If it isn't approved you'll be asked to edit it.

What Google lets you do with a reply

Google's own help page on replying to reviews covers the basics, and a few details matter more than they sound.

  • You must be verified. If your profile isn't verified yet, start with our guide to verifying your Google Business Profile.
  • Replies show as your business, not under your personal name. Write them as the business, then sign off with a first name if you like.
  • Google reviews every reply. Checks usually take up to 10 minutes, though Google says they can occasionally take much longer. A reply that breaks the content policies is sent back for editing.
  • The reviewer is notified and can change their review after reading your reply. A considered reply gives an unhappy customer a reason to look again; an angry one gives them nothing.
  • You can edit or delete a reply later from Read reviews, using the edit or delete icon under it. You can't delete the customer's review itself.

A five-part formula for any reply

Most good replies are two to four sentences. This order works for praise and for complaints, and it stops you rambling or getting defensive.

  1. 1. Thank them, by name"Thanks, Priya" reads like a person. "Thank you for your feedback" reads like a form letter.
  2. 2. Name one specific thingPick a detail from their review (the Yorkshire puddings, the 8am appointment, the leaking tap). It proves a human read it.
  3. 3. Own what went wrongIf something failed, say so in one plain sentence. No excuses, no "but", no blaming a busy day.
  4. 4. Say what happens nextAn apology with no action is just noise. Offer a concrete step, or invite them to contact you directly.
  5. 5. Sign off brieflyA first name and role ("Marcus, owner") is enough. Don't paste your full opening hours or a sales pitch.

12 example replies you can adapt

These are made-up examples, written for small UK businesses, so you can see how the formula sounds in practice. Don't paste them as they are: swap in your own details, because a reply that could belong to any business helps nobody.

Positive reviews

Example · 5 stars · pub, Leeds

Review

Best Sunday roast we've had in Headingley. The beef was perfect and the Yorkshire puddings were huge. Our server Callum looked after the kids brilliantly.

Reply

Thanks so much, Priya. I'll pass your kind words on to Callum, and the kitchen will be thrilled about the Yorkshires: they're made fresh every Sunday morning. Roasts run until 6pm, so we hope to see you and the kids again soon. Marcus, owner

Why it works: It names two details, credits the staff member and ends with an invitation, not a pitch.

Example · 5 stars · hairdresser, Bristol

Review

Finally found someone who gets curly hair. Dani took her time and I walked out with a cut that works with my curls, not against them.

Reply

Thank you, Jess! Dani loves a curly-hair challenge, so this made her week. We cut curls dry first so we can see where they naturally fall, which is why it takes a little longer. See you in about ten weeks for a trim. Amy, Loop & Shear

Why it works: It explains the thing the customer noticed, which shows expertise to the next person reading.

Example · 5 stars · plumber, Manchester

Review

Boiler packed up on a Sunday night. Tom was here within two hours and it was sorted before the kids got up. Fair price too.

Reply

Thanks, Helen. A dead boiler on a Sunday night is no fun, so I'm glad we got you warm again before the school run. If the pressure ever drops again, ring the office and quote your job number and we'll come straight back. Tom Rigby, Rigby Heating

Why it works: It shows reliability without bragging, and gives future customers a reason to trust the warranty.

Mixed reviews

Example · 3 stars · café, Cardiff

Review

Lovely coffee and the cake was great, but we waited 25 minutes for our food on Saturday and nobody told us why.

Reply

Thank you for being honest, Gareth, and sorry about the wait. Saturday late morning is when we're stretched, and we should have told you at the table instead of leaving you guessing. We've put a second person on the pass at weekends. Come in on a weekday, ask for me, and the first coffee is on us. Siân

Why it works: It accepts the real problem (no communication) and offers a fix and a reason to return.

Example · 4 stars · dental practice, Glasgow

Review

Dentist was gentle and explained everything. Only four stars because parking is awful and the waiting room was freezing.

Reply

Thank you for the kind words about the team. We can't discuss anyone's care in a public reply, but your points about parking and the waiting room are useful. We're adding a list of nearby car parks to our appointment reminders, and reception has been asked to check the heating. Glasgow Road Dental Care

Why it works: Health businesses should never confirm someone was a patient. This reply thanks them without doing so.

Example · 3 stars · takeaway, Sheffield

Review

Food was tasty but half of our order was missing when it arrived.

Reply

We're sorry, Dan. A missing half of an order isn't the standard we want. Please message us your order number and the items that didn't arrive, and we'll put it right. Thanks for letting us know about the food too. Nadia, manager

Why it works: It acknowledges the praise and the failure in one breath, and asks for exactly the details needed to fix it.

Review

Why it works: It doesn't argue about money in public and gives a named person to speak to.

Why it works: A serious safety complaint needs no defence. It apologises, shows action and moves to a private conversation.

When not to reply, or to take it offline

  • Never share private details such as full names of other customers, booking details, health information or what treatment someone had.
  • Don't argue the facts in public. One calm correction is fine. A back-and-forth looks worse to a stranger than the original review.
  • Don't reply while you're angry. Write it, leave it for an hour, then read it as a customer would.
  • Don't offer compensation in public unless you'd happily offer it to everyone. Invite them to contact you and sort it privately.
  • Don't reply to a review that breaks Google's rules by arguing. Report it instead (see below).
  • Skip the discount code. A reply is for the next reader as much as for the reviewer, and sales lines make it look like an advert.

Report a review instead of arguing with it

You can't delete a customer's review because you disagree with it. Google says only reviews that violate its policies are eligible for removal, and it doesn't mediate disagreements between businesses and customers. If a review does break the rules, report it from Read reviews: select the report icon beside the review, choose a reason and select Send report. Google's page on reporting reviews explains the appeal route if a report isn't accepted.

How quickly should you reply?

Google doesn't set a deadline, but it does say helpful, positive replies show you're responsive. As a working rule, answer new reviews within a day or two, and answer a serious complaint the same day. Pick a fixed time each week if you struggle to keep up.

It matters because reviews decide who gets the call. In BrightLocal's 2018 survey of US consumers, 57% said they would only use a business with four or more stars. Google also says customers are 70% more likely to visit a business with a complete profile, and replies are part of looking complete.

Frequently asked questions

Can I delete a Google review?

No. You can reply to a review or report it, but only Google can remove it, and only if it violates its policies. Google says it doesn't mediate disagreements between businesses and customers.

Do I have to verify my business before replying?

Yes. Google's help page says you must verify your business before you can reply to reviews.

Does the customer see my reply?

Yes. Approved replies appear publicly under the review, shown as coming from your business, and the reviewer is notified.

Can the customer change their review after I reply?

Yes. Google says reviewers can change their review after reading your reply, and the review date updates to show the latest change.

Should I reply to every review?

It is a good habit to reply to every review that has a comment, and to thank people for star-only reviews briefly. Spend your effort on the negative ones: that's where a reply changes what future customers think.

What if Google rejects my reply?

Google checks replies against its content policies and will ask you to edit one that isn't approved. Remove anything personal, promotional or abusive and submit it again.

How long should a reply be?

Two to four sentences is usually right. Long replies to short reviews look defensive.

Can I use a template?

Use templates as a starting point, never as the finished reply. Customers and Google can both tell when the same text is posted again and again. Add a name and one detail from the review.
