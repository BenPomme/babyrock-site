What optimising a Business Profile really means

Optimising does not mean tricks. Google's page on how to improve your local ranking says local results are based mainly on three factors: relevance (how well your profile matches what someone searched for), distance (how far you are from the searcher) and prominence (how well known you are, helped by links from other websites and by the number of reviews and their ratings).

You cannot change distance, and you cannot buy a better position: the same page states there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. What you control is the information on your profile. Complete, detailed, accurate information helps Google match you to the right searches, and an active profile with recent reviews and photos gives people a reason to choose you once they find you.

So the checklist below is mostly about being complete and truthful. It is grouped in four parts: Basics (the facts), Content (what shows your business), Reviews (what other people say, and how you answer) and Upkeep (keeping it all true). If your profile is not verified yet, start with how to verify your Business Profile, because several features, such as replying to reviews, need verification.

Google changes the features inside a Business Profile from time to time, and some features exist only in certain countries or business categories. If something described here looks different on your screen, follow the wording in your own profile and in Google's help pages.

Basics: the facts Google and customers rely on

These eight points are the foundation. They are all described in Google's guidelines for representing your business and in the page on editing your Business Profile.

1. Business name: exactly as on your signage

Use the real-world name you show consistently on your signage, stationery and website. Google's guidelines say to leave out taglines, store codes, opening hours, phone numbers, URLs and location details such as "near the station". Adding keywords ("Cheap Plumber Leeds") is the classic mistake: it breaks the guidelines and risks suspension. Also avoid writing the name in all capitals. If you change the name after verification, Google may ask you to verify again.

2. Categories: one primary, then only what fits

You pick one primary category and can add up to nine more. Google's advice is to use the fewest categories that describe your core business and to make each as specific as possible. A useful test from the guidelines: the category should complete the sentence "This business is a...", so it says what you are, not what you sell. A hair salon that also sells shampoo is still a hair salon. Do not add the category of a separate business that happens to operate inside yours.

3. Address or service area

Use a precise, real address; P.O. boxes, remote mailboxes and virtual offices are not eligible. Keep one profile per location. If you travel to customers and do not serve people at your premises, hide the address and set a service area instead. Google says a service area should generally stay within about two hours' driving time of your base. A mobile mechanic or a cleaner usually fits this model; a shop with a counter does not.

4 and 5. Regular hours and special hours

List your regular, customer-facing hours and mark days you are closed. Use special hours for bank holidays, a refurbishment or an event, so your normal pattern stays untouched. Some types of business, such as hotels and schools, should not list hours at all, so read the guidelines for your category. Our guide to holiday hours on Google walks through setting them.

6. Phone number

Use a local number that represents your specific location and that you control, not a call-centre line. You can add a primary number and up to two more, mobile or landline but not fax. Premium-rate numbers are not allowed.

7. Website

Give the full address starting with https://, pointing to a page about your business and your location. Google does not accept links that simply redirect people elsewhere, including to a social media page, as your website. You can also add one link per platform for social profiles such as Instagram or Facebook.

8. WhatsApp or chat link

Google lets you add a WhatsApp or text-message option to a verified profile, but only in some regions, so check whether it appears in yours. If it does, test the link from your own phone and make sure someone answers. A chat button that nobody watches is worse than none.

Content: what shows customers what you are like

9. Business description

The description allows up to 750 characters, with no URLs or HTML. Google says to describe your services, products, mission and history honestly and not to include promotions, prices, sales offers or links. Write it for a person: what you do, who for, where, and one detail that makes you different. Put the most important sentence first, since a profile may show only the opening lines.

10 and 11. Photos and videos

Google's page on managing photos and videos sets the technical limits: JPG or PNG files between 10 KB and 5 MB, recommended resolution of 720 by 720 pixels (minimum 250 by 250), in focus, well lit and without heavy filters or edits, so the image represents reality. Videos can run up to 30 seconds, up to 75 MB, at 720p or higher.

The page recommends a logo, a cover photo that represents the business well, and business photos of the storefront, products and services. It specifically suggests an exterior photo, because it helps customers recognise the place when they arrive. Use your own pictures. Customers can tell a stock shot from the real counter.

  • A clear photo of the entrance, taken from the street, so people know where to stop.
  • Your work or products: finished jobs, dishes, haircuts, a tidy workshop.
  • The team, if they are happy to appear, and the inside of the place at its normal busiest.
  • Replace anything out of date, such as an old menu board or a shopfront you have since repainted.

12. Services, menu or products

Food and drink businesses can add a menu; other businesses can list services. Retail businesses in some countries, including the UK and Ireland, can show products through a connected point-of-sale system. List what you really do, in plain words, with prices only if you are sure you will keep them current. Out-of-date prices cause arguments at the till.

13. Attributes

Attributes are facts such as outdoor seating, Wi-Fi, parking or wheelchair access. Tick the ones that are true. Google says subjective attributes, such as "cosy", are based on what Google users say rather than on what you choose, so concentrate on the factual ones.

14. Posts and updates

Google's page on creating and managing posts lists three kinds: updates, offers (with a title, dates and times) and events. Posts older than six months are archived unless you set a date range, which is another reason to post regularly. Keep to the content rules: no offensive material, no links to unsafe sites, and a phone number in a post may be rejected if Google cannot connect it to your business. A short post with a real photo and one clear action is enough.

Reviews: get them, answer them, report the bad ones

Google lists the number of reviews and their ratings as part of prominence, so reviews are both a ranking input and the first thing many customers read.

15. Ask for reviews

Ask every happy customer while the experience is fresh, ideally in the same moment you thank them. A short, personal message works better than a generic request. See how to get more Google reviews and our review request message templates. Never pay for reviews or write your own; our page on fake Google reviews explains why that backfires.

16. Reply to every review

Google's page on managing customer reviews explains how: once your business is verified, open "Read reviews", choose reply, write your answer and submit. Replies appear publicly under the review as coming from your business, and the reviewer is notified and may update their review. Google checks each reply against its content policies first; this usually takes about ten minutes but can occasionally take longer.

Replying to every review, positive or negative, shows that a real person is paying attention. Keep happy-customer replies specific: mention the thing they praised. For unhappy ones, stay calm, be factual and offer to continue the conversation privately. We cover the structure in how to respond to Google reviews and how to respond to negative reviews.

17. Report reviews that break Google's rules

If a review appears to break Google's content policies, such as spam, fake content or abuse, you can flag it from your profile for Google to check. A review you simply disagree with is not necessarily a breach, so reply to it instead. Read how to remove Google reviews to understand what Google will and will not take down.

Upkeep: keeping the profile true

18. A monthly check of the whole profile

Once a month, search for your own business name and look at your profile as a customer would. Check the hours, phone number, website link, photos and the most recent reviews. Open the list of people with access to the profile and remove anyone who should no longer have it. Ten minutes is enough if you do it regularly.

19. Holiday hours, in advance

Set special hours two to three weeks before every bank holiday, local festival, staff holiday or planned closure, and set them back to normal afterwards if you need to. Customers trust the hours they see on Google over a sign on your door, so a wrong entry becomes your problem quickly.

20. Questions and answers, only where supported

Google's edit page says owners and managers can answer customer questions, but only for limited categories and regions. Features like this have changed before. Check whether your profile currently shows a Q&A section; if it does, answer questions and keep the answers short and factual, and if it does not, do not spend time on it.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do first to optimise my Google Business Profile?

Verify the profile, then make the basics correct: your real name, a specific primary category, address or service area, hours, phone and website. These are the details customers and Google rely on most.

How many categories can I add to my Business Profile?

One primary category and up to nine more. Google advises using the fewest categories that describe your core business, each as specific as possible, so add secondary ones only if they genuinely describe what you do.

Can I put keywords in my business name?

No. Google's guidelines say the name should match your real-world name as shown on signage, without taglines or location details. Adding keywords can lead to your profile being suspended.

How long can my business description be?

Up to 750 characters. It cannot contain URLs or HTML, and Google advises against promotions, prices or sales offers. Describe what you do and what makes you different.

What size should my Google Business Profile photos be?

JPG or PNG between 10 KB and 5 MB. Google recommends 720 by 720 pixels, with 250 by 250 as the minimum. Photos should be sharp and well lit without heavy filters.

How often should I post on my Business Profile?

There is no fixed rule from Google. Posts older than six months are archived unless you set dates, so posting every few weeks keeps something current. A short, real update is better than a rushed one.

Do I have to reply to every Google review?

Google does not require it, but replies are public and the reviewer is notified. Answering every review, good or bad, shows customers that someone is listening.
