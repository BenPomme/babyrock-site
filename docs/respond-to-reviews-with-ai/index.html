What AI replies do well

Replying to reviews is a job small businesses put off, because it is repetitive and a bad review makes it feel personal. That is exactly where AI helps. A decent model, given a review and a few instructions, will do these things reliably:

  • Remove the blank page. A first draft in ten seconds is easier to fix than a reply you have to invent while you are annoyed.
  • Keep a calm tone. It does not get defensive at 11pm after a long shift. For a harsh review, a model will usually stay polite where a tired owner might not.
  • Cover the basics every time. Thank the reviewer, refer to what they said, apologise where it fits, invite them to get in touch.
  • Handle volume. If you have a backlog of two hundred unanswered reviews, a draft for each one is realistic. Doing them by hand is not.
  • Write in the reviewer's language. A review in Polish or Spanish can get a reply in the same language, which matters for a business with international customers.

Where AI replies go wrong

The same tools cause three predictable problems. All three are fixable, but only if you know to look for them.

1. Generic text that could belong to any business

Without detail, a model falls back on stock lines: "Thank you for your valuable feedback, we strive to provide excellent service." Nobody reads that and believes a person wrote it. It also repeats across dozens of reviews, which looks like a form letter to anyone scrolling your profile. The fix is to make the reply use something only this reviewer said: the dish, the stylist, the day, the problem.

2. Made-up facts and offers

A model wants to sound helpful, so it may promise things. Typical examples: "We have refunded your order", "The manager has spoken to the chef", "Our kitchen is fully nut-free", "We are open until midnight". If you did not tell it, it does not know. A reply that promises a refund or states a safety claim is a public commitment with your name on it. Anything about allergies, prices, refunds, opening hours or what happened on the day must come from you, not from the model.

3. The wrong tone

Models drift towards corporate warmth: exclamation marks, "We are thrilled", "We sincerely apologise for any inconvenience". A café with a friendly, plain-spoken owner ends up sounding like a bank. The opposite also happens: a joking reply under a review about something that really went wrong. Tone is the part most worth correcting by hand, because regulars know how you actually talk.

A checklist for good AI replies

Whether you use a chat window, a plug-in or a service, hold every AI-written reply to these standards before it is published.

  1. Uses the reviewer's specificsIt mentions at least one real detail from the review. If you could paste the reply under a different review without changing a word, rewrite it.
  2. Sounds like the ownerShort sentences if you write short sentences. No exclamation marks if you never use them. Sign off the way you sign off, with a first name if you normally use one.
  3. Never invents facts or offersNo refunds, discounts, free items, deadlines, staff actions or policies unless you supplied them. If the model does not know, the reply should invite the customer to contact you.
  4. Keeps health and personal data outNo confirming that someone was a patient or client, no treatments, medical conditions, full names of other people, booking details or account information. This matters most for dental, medical, beauty and legal businesses.
  5. Gets human approval for 1 to 3 starsA person reads and approves every complaint before it is published. A wrong word in a reply to a five-star review costs little. In reply to a safety complaint, it can cost a lot.
  6. Stays short and calmTwo to four sentences. One apology, not three. No arguing, no sarcasm, no sales pitch.

A prompt you can paste into any AI chat

You do not need special software to try this. Any general AI chat will do. The difference between a generic reply and a useful one is almost entirely in the instructions you give it. Copy the prompt below, replace the bits in brackets with your own details, and paste the review at the end.

Prompt: You are writing a public reply, as the owner, to a Google review of my business. About me: I run [business type] in [town]. I write in a [warm / plain / dry] tone, in short sentences, with no exclamation marks. I sign off as [name]. Rules: (1) Mention at least one specific thing the reviewer said. (2) Do not invent any fact, offer, refund, discount, policy or action I have not told you. (3) Do not mention health, personal or booking details, and do not confirm the reviewer was a customer if that would reveal private information. (4) If the review is negative, apologise once for what went wrong, do not argue, and invite them to contact me on [phone or email] to sort it out. (5) Two to four sentences. (6) Write in the same language as the review. If you are missing a fact you need, tell me instead of guessing. Here is the review: [paste review]

Here is what it can produce for a real-sounding café review. The owner's instructions were: warm, plain, no exclamation marks, sign off as Nuria.

Example · 3 stars · café, Manchester · AI output

Review

Flat white was spot on and the banana bread is the reason we keep coming back. But we waited a good 20 minutes for toasties on Saturday and nobody said anything.

Reply

Thank you for the kind words about the flat white and the banana bread, I'll pass that on to the bakers. You're right that nobody should wait 20 minutes without a word, and I'm sorry we left you guessing. Saturdays are our busiest time and we should have told you at the table. If you drop me a line on the number on our profile, I'll make sure your next visit goes better. Nuria

Why it works: It uses two details from the review, apologises once, promises nothing the owner did not offer, and stays in a plain voice.

Notice what a careful owner would still check. "I'll pass that on to the bakers" is a small claim, and it is only true if she does. "I'll make sure your next visit goes better" is soft enough to be safe. If the model had written "your next coffee is free", that would be an offer the owner never made, and it would have to come out. Read each draft as the customer will, then publish.

Three ways to improve the output

  • Paste two or three of your own past replies under the prompt and say "match this style". It is the quickest way to get your voice.
  • Tell it what is true. If you do refund late orders, say so. Facts you supply are fine; facts it guesses are not.
  • Ask for two versions, one shorter and one warmer, and pick the one that sounds like you.

A chat window is fine for the odd review. It stops being practical once you have to copy, paste, check and post each reply by hand across every review, every week.

What Google says about automated replies

Google's help page on replying to reviews says replies are checked against its content policies before they appear publicly, and that you will be asked to edit one that is not approved. We could not find a Google help page that bans or allows AI-written replies as such, so the safe reading is this: it does not matter who or what wrote the reply, it has to follow Google's content policies.

Those policies are set out in Google's Prohibited and restricted content page. They cover things such as spam, off-topic or promotional content, personal information and offensive language. In practice that matches the checklist above: keep replies relevant to the review, leave out private details, and do not turn them into adverts or paste the same text under every review.

Whatever tool you use, the reply goes out under your business name. You are responsible for what it says.

Where AI should not decide

  • Refunds, compensation and blame. A model can word an apology, but you decide whether it is yours.
  • Safety and allergy complaints. Always read these yourself and follow up privately.
  • Suspected fake reviews. Do not reply with a long argument. Report them to Google; see our guide to fake Google reviews.
  • Legal threats and anything involving staff misconduct. Reply briefly or not at all, and take it offline.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use ChatGPT or another AI chat to reply to Google reviews?

Yes. Paste the review with clear instructions about your tone, what is true and what to leave out, then read the draft before you post it. Without those instructions you usually get a generic reply.

Does Google allow AI-written replies?

We could not find a Google help page that bans or allows them specifically. Google says replies are checked against its content policies, so whatever wrote the reply, it has to follow those rules.

Will customers know a reply was written by AI?

They can often tell when a reply is generic or repeated across many reviews. A reply that uses the reviewer's details and your usual voice reads like a person wrote it, because a person approved or shaped it.

Is it safe to let AI reply to negative reviews?

Only with a safeguard. A wrong promise or a mention of personal details in a reply to a 1 star review can make things worse. Have a person read every 1 to 3 star reply first, or choose a tool that waits for your OK.

Should AI replies mention health or customer details?
