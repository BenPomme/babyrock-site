How to add your business to Google Maps

A plain guide to getting your business on Google: eligibility, the steps, claiming a listing and verification.

Updated 25 November 2026 · By the BabyRock team

The short answer

Adding your business to Google Maps is free. Go to business.google.com/add, select Add your business to Google, enter your business details and choose a verification option. Only businesses that meet Google's eligibility guidelines can be added. If a listing for your business already exists, claim it from Google Maps instead of creating a second one.

The steps

  1. Check you qualifyGoogle lists who can have a Business Profile. In short, customers must be able to deal with you in person during your stated hours, or you must serve them at their address. See the eligibility guidelines.
  2. Go to business.google.com/addSign in with your Google account and select Add your business to Google. If Google already lists your business, claim it instead (see below).
  3. Enter your business detailsFollow the on-screen instructions. Make sure your address is correct and meets Google's address guidelines.
  4. Choose a verification optionGoogle offers the methods available to your business. Pick one and follow the steps shown.
  5. Wait for the reviewGoogle says a review can take up to five business days, and sometimes longer. Once approved, your business can appear on Maps and Search.

Who can add a business to Google

Google calls it a Business Profile. You may still hear the old name, Google My Business. Only businesses eligible for a profile can add or claim a location, and Google's eligibility guidelines set the line:

  • In-person contact. The business has to make in-person contact with customers during its stated hours. That covers shops, cafes, salons and surgeries, and also tradespeople who visit customers at their address.
  • Not eligible: rental or for-sale properties such as holiday homes or vacant flats, lead-generation companies, and online-only businesses, brands and organisations.
  • Not an address: P.O. boxes and mailboxes in remote locations don't count as a business address.
  • Seasonal businesses qualify if they keep permanent signage at the location all year.
  • Age-restricted businesses (alcohol, cannabis, weapons) can't be service-area businesses without a storefront.

You also need to be the owner or an authorised representative, because only they can verify and manage a profile.

Already listed? Claim it, don't duplicate it

Google often has a listing for a business before the owner does. Creating a second one causes confusion for you and for customers. Following Google's claim guide:

  1. Search Google MapsLook up your business by name and city.
  2. Select Claim this businessThen choose Manage now.
  3. Choose a verification optionFollow the steps on screen.

If the profile is already verified by someone else, you can't claim it with these steps. You have to request ownership from the current owner.

How verification works

Until you verify, you can't manage your profile or reply to reviews. Google picks the methods it offers based on your business type, public information, region and opening hours, and you can't change them. Some businesses need more than one. Our full guide to verifying a Business Profile goes through each one.

MethodWhat happensGood to know
Phone or textGoogle calls or texts a code to your business numberAutomated phone systems can't receive the code
EmailYou follow the steps in an email sent to the address shown on screenYou need access to that inbox
Live video callA support representative guides you on a video callOnly during your business hours, and not offered to everyone
Video recordingYou record a video on your phone and upload itAt least 30 seconds, one live take, no cuts
PostcardGoogle posts a five-digit code to your addressMost arrive within 14 days; the code expires after 30

Google's official videos on video verification

Google's Business Profile channel has two short videos on verifying with a recording, one for businesses with a storefront and one for service-area businesses. They're in English and can be shown with subtitles.

What to complete once you're verified

A complete profile gets noticed. Google says customers are 70% more likely to visit businesses with a complete Business Profile. Google doesn't rank which fields matter most, so start with what customers need before they turn up:

  • Opening hours, including special hours for bank holidays. Our guide shows how to set holiday hours.
  • Phone number and website, matching what's on your door and your invoices.
  • Photos of the outside, the inside and your work.

Frequently asked questions

Is it free to add my business to Google Maps?

Yes. Google says adding or claiming a business on Google is free.

How long does verification take?

Google says a review can take up to five business days, and in rare cases longer. If you choose the postcard method, most codes arrive within 14 days.

Can I add a business without a shop?

Yes, if you serve customers at their address. Google has a process for service-area businesses, and you give your service area instead of showing your address. Online-only businesses aren't eligible.

Do I need a website?

No. You add your business with a Google account and your business details. Add a website when you have one.

What if my business already shows on Google Maps?

Search for it, select Claim this business and then Manage now. Don't create a duplicate.

Why does Google ask for a video?

It's one way to prove the location exists and that you manage the business. Google recommends it when your business is eligible.

What if Google asks for more details?

Google says this is likely because your business details recently changed. Select Get verified and try again.

What's the difference between Google My Business and Google Business Profile?

They're the same service under an older and a newer name. Today you manage it at business.google.com or from Search and Maps.
