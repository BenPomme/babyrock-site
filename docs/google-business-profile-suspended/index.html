Google Business Profile suspended: why it happens and how to appeal

What Google says about suspended and disabled profiles, the usual causes, and the appeal route, in the order to do things.

Updated 25 November 2026 · By the BabyRock team

The short answer

A suspended or disabled profile is one Google has removed because it broke its guidelines. The public can't see it and you can't manage it. Fix the cause first, then submit one appeal in Google's appeals tool with matching evidence. An appeal can only be submitted once, and you should never create a new profile for the same business.

The steps

  1. Read the notice and note the reasonSign in with the Google Account linked to the profile. The appeals tool shows the date of the decision, the reason and a link to the policy involved.
  2. Compare the profile with Google's guidelinesCheck the name, address, category, phone, website and hours against the rules below. Fix anything that doesn't match your real business.
  3. Gather evidenceCollect a company registration, business licence, tax certificate or a business utility bill. The name and address on each must match the profile.
  4. Submit one appealOpen the Google Business Profile appeals tool, pick the profile and the decision, add your evidence and a short explanation, then select Submit appeal.
  5. Wait for the email and check the statusGoogle emails the decision. You can also see the status in the appeals tool: Submitted, Approved or Not Approved.

Suspended or disabled: what Google actually says

Owners use the two words as if they mean different things, and you'll see both in forums. Google's page on fixing suspended or disabled profiles treats them together: it says Google may suspend or disable profiles that don't follow its guidelines, and the same appeal route covers both. Google doesn't publish a strict definition that separates the two, so don't spend time trying to work out which one you have. The reason shown in the appeals tool is what matters.

What happens when a profile is removed, according to that page:

  • The public can't go to the profile, so it won't appear on Search or Maps.
  • The owner and managers can't act on the profile.
  • The owner and managers can ask Google to reinstate it. If it's reinstated, the profile comes back together with its owner and managers.
  • If Google removes an owner's Google Account, every location that account owns is removed. If the owner's account is reinstated, so are those locations.
  • If it removes a manager's account, that manager is suspended on the accounts they manage, but the locations aren't affected.

There is a third case that catches people out. Google's overview of Business Profile policies says that if a merchant shows a pattern of breaking the policies, access can be restricted, and when an account is restricted, every profile associated with it is suspended. A restricted account also can't create or claim other profiles. That is why a suspension sometimes hits several locations at once.

Check first that you're looking at a suspension and not at a profile that was never visible. If the profile has simply never shown up, start with why your business isn't showing on Google Maps.

Google says its guidelines are meant to help you avoid problems that include changes to your information or, in some cases, removal of your business from Google.

What to fix before you appeal

An appeal can only be submitted once, so treat the first one as the only one. Google's own advice on the page is to make sure the profile follows all of the guidelines before you submit. In practice that means going through the profile field by field.

  • Name: exactly as on your sign, shopfront and website. Remove taglines, keywords, opening hours, phone numbers, locations and capital-letter styling that isn't part of the real name.
  • Address: your actual premises, with a street address Google can find. If you work from home and visit customers, hide the address and use a service area instead.
  • Category: the fewest categories that describe your core business, and the most specific one as primary.
  • Phone and website: a number you control and a website for this business, not a call centre or a page that forwards visitors elsewhere.
  • Hours: your regular customer-facing hours. Use special hours for holidays rather than editing your regular hours.
  • Duplicates: search Maps for your phone number and address. If a second profile for the same location exists in your account or another, sort it out first.
  • Other Google accounts: Google says that when a profile was created through an account tied to another Google product, a restriction on that account can lead to suspension of the profile. If you used a personal or old work account, check that account is in good standing.

Then collect evidence. Google lists a company registration, a business licence, tax certificates and business utility bills (electricity, phone, water, internet) as evidence that can strengthen an appeal, and tells you to check that the business name and address on every document match the profile. If your profile says one thing and your registration another, that mismatch is the first thing to fix, not to explain away in the text box.

How to appeal, step by step

This is Google's process from its appeals page, which applies to businesses in the UK and Europe as well. Google says businesses in the UK or the European Economic Area use the appeals tool for a suspended or disabled profile.

  1. Open the Google Business Profile appeals toolIf you're signed in to the Google Account related to the profile, select Confirm. To use another account, select Switch account.
  2. Select the profile, then ContinueIf you manage several, choose the one you want to appeal.
  3. Select the decision you want to appeal, then ContinueThe tool shows details such as the date of the decision, the reason, a link to the policy that was violated, whether it can be appealed and its status.
  4. Add evidence if you have itSelect Choose file to upload your documents. You can attach several files. The name and address on each should match the profile.
  5. Explain why the profile should be reinstatedUse the text box. Be specific and brief: what the business is, where it operates, and what you corrected.
  6. Tick the confirmation box and select Submit appealThe box says you're ready to submit and that all optional supporting evidence is attached. Remember that appeals can only be submitted once.

What to write in the explanation box

Google doesn't give a template. Keep it short and factual. Something along these lines, adapted to your case: "Our name was shown with a service description added. We have corrected it to the name on our shopfront sign and invoices. The address is our premises at the address shown, and the attached electricity bill and registration show the same name and address." State what you changed and point to the evidence. Don't write about how much the profile matters to you, and don't say the reviewer made a mistake if you can't show it.

How long it takes and what happens next

Google says appeal reviews and decisions can take up to 5 business days and that you shouldn't submit multiple appeals for the same issue before you get a decision. It will email you the decision. You can also open the appeals tool, select the profile, and read the status on the right: Submitted, Approved, Not Approved, Can't be appealed or Eligible for appeal.

If the account is restricted

If your Google Account is restricted for violating policy, the profiles you manage are suspended and you can't create or claim others. Google says to appeal the account first, with a reason, on your My accounts page, where you can track it. Once the restriction is lifted, you can then submit an appeal for the profile itself.

If the appeal is denied

Google says that only if your reinstatement request is denied, it may be able to do an additional review, and you can submit evidence that wasn't included in the original appeal. So don't use up the evidence in the first appeal if a clearer document is on its way. If your business is in the European Economic Area, Google also notes you may have additional redress options, including referring the appeal to an out-of-court dispute settlement body. Its page on appealing profile and content restrictions explains those routes.

More than 10 locations

Google has a bulk route: if you manage more than 10 suspended profiles, the appeals tool asks whether you're requesting bulk reinstatement, and you attach a spreadsheet containing the Business Profile ID of each location together with your supporting documents. The status only updates for the profile you selected, and you receive one email for it.

What never to do

  • Don't create a new profile for the same business. Google says it twice on its appeals pages. A second profile for the same location is itself against the guidelines.
  • Don't submit several appeals. Google asks you not to send multiple appeals for the same issue before receiving a decision, and each decision can only be appealed once.
  • Don't edit the profile in a rush while it's suspended. Google says content added to a suspended or disabled profile may be rejected until it's reinstated. Make your corrections, but expect them to take effect only after reinstatement.
  • Don't keyword-stuff the name or the categories. Adding "emergency plumber 24 hours London" to a business called Hartley Plumbing is exactly the unnecessary information Google lists as grounds for suspension. The same goes for using categories solely as keywords.
  • Don't hand over fake documents. Evidence has to match the real business. Google tells you to check that names and addresses match, so a mismatch gives the reviewer an easy reason to say no.
  • Don't pay anyone who promises guaranteed reinstatement. The only route Google describes is its appeals tool and, where it offers it, a further review.
  • Don't rename a profile into a different business. Google says that when a different business now operates at the location, the old one should be marked permanently closed rather than renamed.

There's one legitimate case for a new profile: Google says that if your business has genuinely relocated, you mark the existing profile as permanently closed and create a new one at the new location. That's a move, not a suspension workaround.

After reinstatement: keeping the profile safe

If you have issues with a reinstated profile, Google says to contact Google Business Profile Help. Beyond that, most repeat problems come from edits: a new manager changing the name, a freelancer adding keywords, a tidy-up of categories. Keep one person responsible for edits, and review the name, address and category whenever someone else has had access. Google's page on adding, changing or removing users explains how to manage who can edit.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between a suspended and a disabled Google Business Profile?

Google doesn't publish a strict definition. Its help page covers both together: the public can't see the profile, you can't manage it, and the same appeals tool applies. Check the reason and policy link shown in the tool.

How long does a Google Business Profile appeal take?

Google says appeal reviews and decisions can take up to 5 business days, and it emails you the decision. Don't submit more than one appeal for the same issue while you wait.

Can I appeal more than once?

No. Google says appeals can only be submitted once. If the appeal is denied, it may be able to do an additional review if you provide evidence that wasn't in your original appeal.

Can I just create a new profile for my business?

No. Google says not to create a new profile for the same business. The exception is a genuine move: mark the old location permanently closed and create a profile at the new address.

What evidence helps an appeal?

Google lists a company registration, business licence, tax certificate or a business utility bill such as electricity, phone, water or internet. The business name and address on each document must match the profile.

Why were all my profiles suspended at once?

Google says that if your account is restricted for repeated policy violations, all the profiles associated with it are suspended. Appeal the account first on the My accounts page, then each profile.

Does the appeals tool work for UK and European businesses?

Yes. Google says businesses in the UK or the European Economic Area use the appeals tool for suspended or disabled profiles. EEA businesses may also have out-of-court dispute settlement options.

Will I get my reviews back if the profile is reinstated?

Google says that when it reinstates a profile, it reinstates the profile and its owner and managers. Its page doesn't list reviews separately, so check the profile once it's back.
