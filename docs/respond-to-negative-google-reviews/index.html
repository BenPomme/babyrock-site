How to respond to negative Google reviews

A calm four-step method, ten before and after replies, and the lines you should never cross in public.

Updated 25 November 2026 · By the BabyRock team

The short answer

Wait until you've cooled down, check what actually happened, then reply once in a short, calm message. Thank the customer, own whatever is true, say what you've done or will do, and move any detail offline with a phone number or email. Never argue, never name or describe the customer, and never offer anything in return for changing the review.

The steps

  1. Pause before you typeDon't reply while you're angry. Read the review twice, then step away. A reply written an hour later is almost always better than one written in a minute.
  2. Check the factsLook up the booking, the till record, the job sheet or ask the staff who were there. Decide what is true, what is a misunderstanding and what you can't verify.
  3. Write one short, calm replyThank them, own what is true, say what you're doing about it and keep the tone friendly. Three to five sentences is plenty.
  4. Take the detail offlineGive a direct phone number or email so the real conversation happens in private. If they update their review after you've put it right, thank them once.

Who you are really writing for

You will almost never change the mind of the person who wrote a one-star review. The reply is for everyone who reads it later when they are choosing where to go. Someone scanning your profile sees the complaint, then sees how you handled it. A calm, specific reply tells them you are reasonable. A defensive one tells them to book somewhere else.

Google's own guidance says that replying shows customers you value their feedback, and that negative reviews can highlight areas for improvement and are not always a sign of poor service. It also tells you what happens next: the customer is notified when you reply, and they can still change their review afterwards. So a good reply is not just PR. It is the one thing that can still move the star rating. See Google's Manage customer reviews page.

This page covers the hard cases. For the basics of replying, including where to click, see our guide on how to respond to Google reviews.

The four-step method in detail

Step 1: Pause

The first draft of a reply to an unfair review is nearly always a bad idea. It is fine to write it. Just don't post it. Put the draft in a notes app, leave it, and come back after a break. If you run a team, agree that nobody replies to a one-star review in the same shift it arrived.

Step 2: Check what happened

Before you say anything, find out. Pull the booking, the order, the invoice or the job sheet. Ask the person who served them. You are sorting the review into one of four piles:

  • It's true. You got something wrong. Own it.
  • It's partly true. The experience was poor, but the cause was not quite what they say. Acknowledge the experience and give the missing context without blaming them.
  • It's a misunderstanding. They expected something you never offered. Explain what you do offer, politely, once.
  • You can't match it to a customer. Say so, and invite them to get in touch. If it looks fake, read how to spot and report fake Google reviews before you reply.

Step 3: Write the reply

A reply that works has the same four parts every time, in this order:

  • Thank them for telling you, using their first name if it is shown.
  • Own what is true. One sentence, no excuses attached. 'We got that wrong' beats 'We're sorry if you felt'.
  • Say what you've done or will do. Something concrete: a changed process, a conversation with the team, a refund you have already agreed privately.
  • Give a route offline. A named person, a phone number or an email address.

Keep it short. Long replies read as defensive. Sign it with a first name and your role so it sounds like a person and not a department.

Step 4: Take the detail offline and follow up

Anything that needs account details, dates of treatment, payment information or a back-and-forth belongs in a private conversation. Your public reply is the door. The room behind it is a phone call or an email. Then actually pick up the phone when it rings. If the customer later updates the review, see the section on follow-up below.

Ten before and after replies

Each example below shows the review, the defensive reply many owners write first (in the explanation underneath) and a better version. The names are made up. The situations are ones you will recognise.

Service and delivery

Example · 1 star · takeaway, Sheffield

Review

Ordered at 7pm on a Friday. Pizza turned up at ten to nine, stone cold. Waste of money. Won't be ordering again.

Reply

Thank you for telling us, Dan. Over an hour and a cold pizza is not what we want to send out, and Friday evening is when it matters most. I've looked at that night's tickets and our oven queue backed up while we were short of one driver. We've changed how we stop taking orders when the queue is full. Please ring me on [phone] and I'll put your order right. Mo, manager

Why it works: **Before:** 'It was a busy Friday, we had hundreds of orders. What do you expect? Check the app for delivery times next time.' That blames the customer and sounds like an excuse. The better reply names the real cause, says what changed and offers a private way to fix it.

Example · 1 star · garage, Norwich

Review

Quoted me one price on the phone and the bill was nearly double. Nobody called to ask before doing the extra work. Felt like I was held to ransom.

Reply

Thanks for raising this, Mr Aziz. You should have had a call before any extra work was done, and that is on us. We've since changed our job card so nothing beyond the quote is started without a phone call and a note on the sheet. I'd like to go through your invoice line by line with you. Please ring Hannah on [phone] or email [email address]. Hannah, Service Manager

Why it works: **Before:** 'You signed the work order. Read it before you sign next time.' It may be technically right and still lose the argument in public. The better reply accepts the communication failure, which is the real complaint, and offers an invoice review.

Example · 2 stars · plumber, Cardiff

Review

Booked for a Tuesday morning. Waited in until 1pm, no call, no visit. Had to take a day off for nothing.

Reply

Hello Carys, I'm sorry. Taking a day off and being left waiting is exactly what we are meant to prevent. I've checked our diary and your visit was moved after an earlier job overran, but nobody rang you, which was our mistake. We now text every customer if a visit is going to be more than 30 minutes late. If you're still waiting for the repair, please call me on 029 2018 0000 and I'll fit you in first on the next free morning. Rob

Why it works: **Before:** 'We came at 10 and you weren't in. Not our fault.' If that is true, say it calmly and offline. If you can't prove it, you've called a customer a liar in public. The better reply deals with the part you can check, the missing call.

Staff and attitude

Example · 1 star · restaurant, Brighton

Review

Our waiter was rude and rolled his eyes when we asked for the bill to be split. Ruined my partner's birthday.

Reply

I'm sorry your partner's birthday was spoiled, Emma. That isn't how we want anyone to be treated, and splitting a bill is a normal request. I've spoken to the team about Saturday night and we're reviewing how we handle large tables. If you'd like to tell me more, I'm at [email address] and I read every message myself. Sam, General Manager

Why it works: **Before:** 'Our staff are never rude. You were loud and clearly had too much to drink.' That accuses the customer with no proof and calls them a liar. The better reply believes the feeling, deals with the issue internally and invites a private chat.

Example · 2 stars · hotel, York

Review

Room smelt damp and the bathroom wasn't clean when we arrived. Reception shrugged when we mentioned it.

Reply

Thank you for flagging this, Priya. A damp smell and an unclean bathroom are both things we should have caught before you arrived, and reception should have helped you on the spot. We've checked that room, and our housekeeping sign-off now includes a smell and bathroom check before check-in. If you can email [email address] with your dates, I'd like to look into what happened at the desk. Tom Ellis, Owner

Why it works: **Before:** 'For the price you paid, what did you expect, the Ritz?' Sarcasm about price makes every reader assume you take guests for granted. The better reply names both problems and what changed.

Example · 3 stars · hairdresser, Leeds

Review

The colour came out much warmer than I wanted and nobody checked with me halfway. Cut was lovely though.

Reply

Thank you, Aisha, and I'm glad the cut works for you. A colour that comes out warmer than you hoped is disappointing, and we should have checked in halfway. We now test the shade at the roots part way through and ask the client before we go on. Come in for a free colour consultation and we'll talk about toning it down. Jo, Loop and Shear

Why it works: **Before:** 'We showed you the swatch and you agreed. Photos on file prove it.' Even if that's true, quoting 'proof' to a client who is unhappy makes you look like you are preparing for court. The better reply admits the missing check and offers a fix.

Situations with a catch

Example · 1 star · dental practice, Glasgow

Review

Waited 40 minutes past my appointment time and nobody apologised. Reception was cold with me.

Reply

Thank you for telling us. We can't discuss anyone's appointment in a public reply, but no one should wait that long without an apology, and that is something we take seriously. We're reviewing how reception tells people about delays. If you'd like to talk it through, please call our practice manager on [phone]. Glasgow Road Dental Care

Why it works: **Before:** 'You were late for your last two appointments and you needed extra time with the hygienist.' That confirms the person was a patient and shares details of their care, which a health business must never do in public. The better reply neither confirms nor denies who they are.

Example · 1 star · gym, Manchester

Review

Tried to cancel and was told I still had to pay another month. Nobody explained this when I joined. Feels like a trap.

Reply

Thank you for raising this, Luke. It's frustrating to find a notice period you didn't expect. I can't go into account details here, but I'd like to look at what you were told when you joined and see whether we can make this right. Please email [email address] with your name and the email you joined with, and I'll reply personally. Hannah, Membership Manager

Why it works: **Before:** 'It's in the contract you signed. Terms are terms.' The review isn't about the contract. It is about feeling tricked. The better reply stays kind, avoids account details in public and goes straight to a person who can help.

Example · 1 star, no comment · café, Edinburgh

Review

(One star, no text.)

Reply

Hello, thank you for the rating. We're sorry we didn't get it right and would really like to know what went wrong. If you can tell us more, please email [email address] or ring us on [phone], and we'll take it from there. Iona, owner

Why it works: **Before:** 'Whoever you are, if you've got a problem, say what it is. A star with no reason is useless.' That sounds annoyed, and it makes the next reader think the problem is you. The better reply is two lines, keeps the door open and doesn't pretend to know what happened.

Example · 2 stars · florist, Bath

Review

Wedding flowers arrived looking tired and two of the table arrangements were missing. We had to improvise on the day.

Reply

I'm so sorry, Chloe. Flowers are meant to make the day easier, not harder, and I can see how stressful it was to find them like that. I've reread our delivery notes and I'd like to talk through what happened in person. Please ring me on [phone] and we'll sort out a fair resolution together. Marion, Bath Blooms

Why it works: **Before:** 'Take this review down and we'll refund you in full.' Google's policy bans offering anything in exchange for a revision or removal of a review, and it can get you flagged. The better reply offers a real conversation about a real problem, and keeps any refund separate from the review.

What never to write in a public reply

Everything you write is public, searchable and can be screenshotted. A few lines are worth refusing to cross, whatever the review said.

  • Personal data. No full names of customers, phone numbers, addresses, order or booking details, or anything that identifies someone beyond what they chose to show. Google's content policy bans posting personal information about other people without consent. See Prohibited and restricted content.
  • Health details. If you run a clinic, dental practice, salon doing treatments, therapy or anything near health, don't confirm that the reviewer was a patient or client, and never mention treatment, conditions or missed appointments. Thank them and invite them to call.
  • Arguments and accusations. 'You are lying', 'you were drunk', 'you're a competitor' and 'you never even came in' are claims you may not be able to prove, and they are the fastest way to turn a one-star review into a story.
  • Sarcasm and snark. It reads as contempt, even if it feels like a clever line when you type it.
  • Offers in exchange for changing the review. Google's policy says merchants must not offer incentives, such as payment, discounts or free goods or services, in exchange for posting a review or for revising or removing a negative one. Resolve the problem on its own merits, and don't link it to the review.
  • Threats, including legal ones. If you think something is defamatory or an extortion attempt, say nothing legal in public and take proper advice. We don't give legal advice.
  • Copy-and-paste text that ignores the review. A generic 'We value your feedback' under a specific complaint tells readers nobody read it.

When to take it offline

Offline doesn't mean you ignore the review. It means that after one public reply, the detail moves somewhere private. Do it when:

  • the fix needs account, booking, payment or health information
  • there is a refund, replacement or re-do to arrange
  • the facts are disputed and you need to compare records
  • the customer is clearly upset and a call will do more than another written message
  • the matter involves a complaint that could become a formal claim

Write the offline route in the reply: a named person and either a direct phone number or an email address that someone reads. Don't send people to a generic contact form and hope. When they get in touch, do what you said. If it goes quiet, a short follow-up call is better than a second public message.

Keep a note of each case: what the review said, what you found, what you offered. It helps if the same issue appears twice, and it is useful if you later decide to report the review.

When to report instead of reply

A review you dislike is not the same as a review that breaks Google's rules. Google says plainly that you shouldn't report a review just because you disagree with it, and that it doesn't get involved in conflicts between businesses and customers. But some reviews do cross the line, and for those a report is the right move, sometimes alongside a short reply.

Consider reporting when a review contains:

  • profanity or obscene language
  • threats, harassment or hate speech
  • personal information about you or your staff, such as a full name posted without consent
  • unsubstantiated allegations of criminal wrongdoing
  • content that isn't based on a real experience, including a review from a competitor or someone with a conflict of interest
  • spam, adverts or off-topic content
  • a demand for money to take the review down. This is an extortion scam: Google has a separate page, Report negative review extortion scams

The steps, the status check and the one-time appeal are in our guide to removing Google reviews. If you believe a review is fake, start with fake Google reviews. Reporting does not guarantee removal. Google reviews the report against its policy, and if the review stays you still have the reply.

A useful rule: if the review is rude but describes a real experience, reply. If it is not about a real experience or breaks a content rule, report. If you're unsure, report and reply once, calmly, in case it stays up.

If the customer updates their review

Google tells customers they can change their review after reading your reply, and that when they do, the date on the review changes to show the latest update. That is the best outcome of a bad review. It tends to follow a reply that is honest and a problem that has really been fixed.

  • Notice it. Check your recent reviews for edits, especially a week or two after a difficult one.
  • Thank them once. A short line is enough: 'Thank you for coming back to us, Dan. We're glad we could put it right.' Don't gloat and don't ask them to post more.
  • Don't ask for a higher rating. Pressuring someone to change a score can break Google's rules on discouraging or influencing reviews and puts you back in a worse place.
  • If they don't change it, leave it. A three-star review with a calm reply and a fixed problem still looks good to the next reader.
  • If they get worse, step back. Don't reply a third time. A second public reply to the same person rarely helps.

Whatever happens to the score, change something real in your business. Patterns in negative reviews, such as the same wait time, the same missing call or the same unclear charge, are the cheapest feedback you will get.

Frequently asked questions

Should I reply to every negative review?

Yes, in most cases. A short, calm reply helps the next reader as well as the reviewer. The exceptions are reviews that break Google's rules, which you can report, and extortion attempts, which Google asks you to report separately. If you aren't sure, reply once, calmly, and take the detail offline.

How quickly should I respond to a bad review?

Quick enough to look attentive, slow enough to be calm. Give yourself time to check the facts first. Google says replies are usually reviewed within about 10 minutes, though it can take up to 30 days.

Can I ask the customer to remove a negative review?

You can ask what went wrong and try to fix it. Don't offer payment, discounts, free goods or services in exchange for editing or removing a review. Google's policy bans merchants from doing that.

What if the review is unfair or untrue?

Reply calmly with the facts you can verify and don't call the person a liar. If the review breaks Google's content policy, for example because it isn't based on a real experience, report it. Reports are checked against the policy, and there is a one-time appeal if the review stays.

Can I mention that the person was a customer or patient?

Avoid it, especially in health-related businesses. Replying 'we have your records' or mentioning an appointment confirms who they are and can reveal private information. Thank them and invite them to contact you.

Does replying change the star rating?

Not by itself. The customer is notified of your reply and can change their review, and some do. Others leave it. Even then, a good reply improves how the review looks to everyone else.

Should I use a template for negative reviews?

A template can give you a starting structure, but a negative review needs at least one specific detail from the complaint. Use our [review response templates](/google-review-response-templates/) as a base and change the facts.
