Google Business Profile video verification: what to film

The exact requirements, a shot list for each type of business, and what to do if Google rejects your video.

Updated 25 November 2026 · By the BabyRock team

The short answer

For video verification, record one continuous, unedited video of at least 30 seconds, live on your phone through your Business Profile. Show where the business is, that it exists, and that you run it. Google reviews it within up to five business days. If it's rejected, select Review issues and record a new one.

The steps

  1. Open Get verifiedOn your phone, open your Business Profile, tap Get verified and add your phone number if you're asked. Tap Next.
  2. Choose Business videoTap Business video, then Next. Read the requirements and allow location, camera and microphone access if prompted.
  3. Record in one takeTap Next, then Start recording. Show the location, the business and proof you run it, without stopping, for at least 30 seconds.
  4. Review and uploadWatch it back. Tap Try again to re-record, or tap Upload video.
  5. Wait for the resultGoogle says review can take up to five business days. You'll be told whether you're verified or why not.

What video verification is, and who can use it

Video verification is one of the ways Google lets you prove that a business is real and that you're allowed to manage its Business Profile. Instead of waiting for a postcard, you film your business with your phone and upload the video through your profile. Google's video verification help page recommends it when your business is eligible, and its general verification page says the same.

You can't pick this method yourself. Google decides which methods to show you, based on your business type, public information, region and opening hours, and sometimes asks for more than one. If you open Get verified and you see Business video, you're eligible. If you don't see it, you'll see whichever methods Google has chosen instead.

One firm limit: online-only businesses aren't eligible for a Business Profile, so they can't be verified by video either. Google's page describes three kinds of eligible business, and each has its own list of things the video has to show:

  • Storefront businesses: you serve customers in person at a physical location with permanent signage, such as a shop, a café or a salon.
  • Hybrid businesses: the same, and you also visit or deliver to customers. Google's example is a dine-in restaurant that offers delivery.
  • Service-area businesses: you go to your customers rather than serving them at your address, such as a locksmith or a plumber. Google says that if you don't serve customers at your business location, you shouldn't add that address to your profile.

The rules for the recording

These apply to every business, whichever type you are. They're the most common reason a video fails before anyone even looks at what's in it.

  • Live only. Google says you must capture the footage live, because pre-recorded videos aren't allowed. You can't film something earlier and upload it later.
  • One continuous take. The video must be unedited and unique, with no breaks and no cuts.
  • At least 30 seconds.
  • On a mobile device, recorded and uploaded through your Business Profile.
  • Nothing sensitive. Don't show bank account numbers, tax or ID numbers, private information about you or anyone else, or other people's faces.

That last rule matters in a busy shop or restaurant. Film when the room is quiet, or frame the shot so you don't catch customers or staff. If someone walks into frame, don't edit them out afterwards: edits aren't allowed. Stop, tap Try again and start over.

The three things your video must show

Whatever kind of business you run, Google is looking for the same three things: where the business is, that it exists, and that you manage it. The wording differs by business type, so here is what Google lists for each.

Shot list: a shop, café or restaurant

Google doesn't prescribe an order, so this is a sequence that covers its three requirements in one natural walk. It's our suggestion, built from what Google lists. Aim to keep the phone moving smoothly and to say nothing you wouldn't want on file.

  1. Start in the streetStand a little way from the entrance and show a street sign, a building number or the shop next door. This is your location shot. A recognisable landmark helps if the street is quiet.
  2. Pan to the frontTurn to the storefront and hold on the sign, the window lettering or the wall where your business name appears. Check it reads exactly as your Business Profile name does.
  3. Walk inCross the threshold in the same take. Show the inside of the shop, dining room or counter, so it's clear this is a real, operating place.
  4. Show you run itGo behind the counter: open the till, step into the kitchen, show the storeroom, or ring something through the point-of-sale system. Google's examples are tasks only employees can do.
  5. Finish cleanlyStop the recording once you've covered everything. Review it before you upload.

A restaurant that also delivers counts as hybrid, and the same list applies. You don't need to film the delivery side. The kitchen is stronger proof than a bag on a counter.

Shot list: a service-area business

You don't have a storefront, so the video has to do more work to show the business is real. Google asks for an operating area, proof the business exists, and proof of management.

  1. Show the areaFilm a street sign or landmark near your business address. Google warns against empty land and places with no clear markers, so pick a spot that someone could find on a map.
  2. Show the tools of the tradeFilm your equipment, products, workspace or branded clothing. A plumber could show a van rack and tool bag; a cleaner could show supplies and uniform; a locksmith could show the key-cutting kit. Business cards in shot are another option Google lists.
  3. Do something only you can doPerform part of a service, or open something that belongs to the business, such as unlocking a branded van. Google also accepts a business permit, an invoice or a utility bill that matches your profile name, held up so the name is readable.
  4. Check the nameIf any signage, clothing or document shows a name, it should match your Business Profile exactly.

Shot list: a home-based business

Many home-based businesses are service-area businesses, and Google says they can show landmarks and signs in the immediate neighbourhood. That means you don't have to film inside your house to show where you are.

  • Location: step outside and film the street sign at the end of your road, or a landmark close to your address. Don't linger on your front door if you'd rather not.
  • Existence: show your workspace, your tools or products, a branded item such as an apron, a van or packaging, and business cards.
  • Management: show yourself doing a piece of the work, or an invoice or business permit with the business name on it. Keep any account numbers or personal details out of frame, as Google asks.
  • Privacy: nothing in the rules says you have to show your home's interior. Frame the workspace and leave the rest out.

If your business serves customers only at their own premises, remember Google's rule that you shouldn't add an address to your profile if you don't serve customers there. That's a profile setting rather than a video question, but it decides which type of video requirements apply to you.

Upload, review time and what happens next

After you finish, you can watch the video back. Tap Try again to re-record, or tap Upload video. If an upload fails, Google says to try uploading again.

Google says review can take up to five business days. The outcome is one of two things: you're notified that the business is verified, or the video isn't accepted, the reasons are shared and you can record a new one.

Google also lets you delete your videos. In the app go to More, then Business Profile settings, then Advanced settings, and under Video uploads tap Delete videos. Google's page says it deletes the video later if you don't. It also says that cancelling an upload removes access to it for both you and Google.

Why videos get rejected, and how to retry

Google lists three example reasons for rejection:

  • The business name isn't visible on the storefront. The name has to appear on a permanent fixture and match your profile.
  • No nearby area is shown. You need street signs, numbers, neighbouring businesses or landmarks.
  • There's no proof you're authorised to run the business. You need to be seen doing something only the business does.

If your video isn't accepted you'll see a Review issues notification on your profile. Here's how to retry:

  1. Tap Review issuesRead each issue Google lists.
  2. Plan the new takeGoogle says to include everything from your first video, plus the information that was missing. Don't film only the missing part.
  3. Record again, liveThe same rules apply: live, continuous, unedited, at least 30 seconds.
  4. Upload the new videoThen wait for the review again.

If it keeps failing, Google's advice is to check that the video meets every requirement and try again. It also suggests trying another verification method before contacting support. Method details are on our guide to every verification method. If the code or the result simply never arrives, see what to do when Google verification isn't working.

Before you film: a short checklist

  • Does your profile name match the sign? If your sign says one thing and your profile says another, fix the profile later, not now. Google warns against changing business details during some verification steps, so check the instructions for your method first.
  • Is your phone charged, with space free? A video that stalls mid-take has to be started again.
  • Have you allowed location, camera and microphone access? Google asks for all three.
  • Is the shop quiet? Fewer people in frame means fewer faces to avoid.
  • Do you have your proof to hand? The till key, the van keys, the invoice or the permit.
  • Are you open? The video itself isn't tied to opening hours. The live video call method is, which is a different option.

Frequently asked questions

How long does Google video verification take?

Google says review can take up to five business days. On rare occasions, Google says, reviews can take longer.

How long does the video have to be?

At least 30 seconds, in one continuous take. Google's page gives a minimum but no stated maximum.

Can I upload a video I filmed earlier?

No. Google says you must capture the footage live and that pre-recorded videos aren't allowed. It has to be unedited too.

Can I verify by video if I work from home?

Yes, if Google offers you the Business video method. Google says home-based businesses can show landmarks and signs in the immediate neighbourhood.

What if my business name isn't on a sign?

For storefronts, Google asks for the name on a permanent fixture such as a sign, wall or window, matching your profile. A rejection for this reason is one of Google's examples.

Where is the Business video option?

Open your Business Profile on your phone, tap Get verified and look for Business video. If it isn't listed, Google hasn't offered it for your business.

What should I do if my video is rejected?

Tap Review issues on your profile, read the reasons and record a new video with everything from the first plus what was missing.

Can online-only businesses verify by video?

No. Google says online-only businesses aren't eligible for a Business Profile, so they can't be verified by video.
