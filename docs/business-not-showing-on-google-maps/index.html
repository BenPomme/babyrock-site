Why your business isn't showing on Google Maps

A checklist of the causes Google's own help pages describe, and how to fix each one.

Updated 25 November 2026 · By the BabyRock team

The short answer

If your business isn't showing on Google Maps, check in order: whether the profile exists and is verified, whether it was suspended or hidden as a duplicate, whether it's marked closed, and whether your category, address and service area are right. If it shows by name but not by service, that's ranking, not a missing profile.

The steps

  1. Search for your exact business name and townIn Google Maps, type the name as it appears on your sign plus the town. If you appear, your profile exists, and the issue is ranking or a wrong detail. If you don't, keep going.
  2. Sign in at business.google.comUse the Google Account linked to the profile. Look for a Get verified button, a suspension notice, or a duplicate warning.
  3. Check status, category, address and hoursConfirm the profile is verified, isn't marked closed, and that the primary category, address and service area describe what you really do.
  4. Search for your own address and phone numberA second listing for your business, or an old one someone else manages, is a common reason your own profile is hidden.
  5. Fix what you foundUse the table below to match the symptom to the fix, then give Google time to review the change.

1. The profile isn't verified, or verification is pending

Google's help page on editing your profile says that to appear on Search, Maps and other Google services you first add or claim your business and then verify it. Its page on improving local ranking adds that verification tells Google you're authorised to represent the business, which makes it more likely to appear in results.

So if you added a business a day ago and it isn't showing, the likely reason is simply that it isn't verified. Google says a verification review can take up to five business days, and on rare occasions longer. Some profiles are verified instantly: when you verify your website through Google Search Console, or when bulk verification is used.

  • Sign in at business.google.com and look for the Get verified button.
  • Pick one of the methods Google offers. You can't choose a method it doesn't show you.
  • If you've asked for a postcard, don't change your name, address or category while you wait, and don't request a second code.
  • If the Get verified button comes back after you finished, Google couldn't fully verify the business. Try again or use another method.

2. The profile was suspended or disabled

Google's page on fixing suspended or disabled profiles says it may suspend or disable profiles that don't follow its guidelines. When a profile is removed, the public can't see it and the owner and managers can't manage it. If your account is restricted for a policy violation, the profiles you manage are suspended as well, and you can't create or claim other profiles.

A profile that appeared normally yesterday and has now disappeared, with a notice when you sign in, points to this. What Google tells you to do:

  • Check that the profile follows all the guidelines first, because an appeal can only be submitted once.
  • Gather optional evidence such as a business registration, business licence, tax certificate or a utility bill. The business name and address on each document must match the profile.
  • Open the Google Business Profile appeals tool, sign in with the account linked to the profile, select the profile and the decision you want to appeal, add your evidence and explanation, and select Submit appeal.
  • Don't create a new profile for the same business. Google warns against it.
  • If your account itself is restricted, lift the restriction first through the My accounts page, then appeal the profile.

Google doesn't give a review time on that page. It says it will email you the decision. If the appeal is denied, you may be able to request an additional review by submitting new evidence that wasn't in the first appeal.

3. Google treats it as a duplicate

A business can have only one profile. Google's page on duplicate profiles and ownership issues says a profile considered a duplicate won't show on Google Search or Maps. It lists three common triggers: a verified profile for the same business already exists, several profiles share an address, or one business has separate profiles for different services.

How to check

Search Maps for your phone number, your address and your business name with variations (with and without "Ltd", an old name, a former address). If another listing comes up that looks like yours, that's the one Google may be showing instead.

What Google says to do

  • Someone else already owns a verified profile for your business: request ownership. The owner has three days to respond. See the section on requesting access below.
  • An unverified profile exists on Maps: claim it and verify it to become the owner.
  • You made a duplicate yourself: you can remove it without affecting your verified listing. Removing deletes its content and linked managers, so double-check you're removing the right one.
  • Two listings, same business: suggest a merge on Google Maps. Google reviews every request. Reviews are combined, but replies to reviews may be lost, and the profiles must represent the same business with the same information.
  • You moved: don't create a new profile. Edit the address on the existing one.
  • Wrongly marked as a duplicate: contact support to appeal. The two businesses must be eligible and distinct. If both operate from the same location, you need evidence of permanent signage showing both businesses.

Several businesses can share an address if they have different names and clearly visible differences in signage. That covers separate brands, departments and independent practitioners in a shared space.

4. You show up by name, but not when people search by service

This is the most misunderstood case, and it often isn't a fault at all. Google's local ranking page says local results are based mainly on three things:

  • Relevance: how well your profile matches what someone is searching for. More detailed, complete information helps.
  • Distance: how far you are from the person searching. If they haven't shared their location, Google uses what it knows about it.
  • Prominence: how well known you are, based on things like links from other websites and the number and rating of your reviews.

Google also states that there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. A newly created profile with few reviews and a thin description is simply less prominent than a long-established one, so it can be missing from a broad search like "bakery" while appearing instantly when someone types its name.

Because distance matters, the same search can give different results from your shop, from your home and from the other side of town. Test as a customer would: from a phone, signed out or in a private window, searching the service plus the town.

What Google recommends for improving visibility:

  • Verify the business.
  • Keep your information complete and current: address, opening hours including special hours, category and attributes such as parking or Wi-Fi.
  • Respond to reviews.
  • Add photos and videos.

5. Category, address and service-area settings

A profile can exist, be verified and still fail to appear where you expect because of how it's set up. Google's edit your profile page sets out the rules.

  • Primary category: pick the one that describes your core business. Google says not to use categories solely as keywords or to copy nearby businesses. You can add up to nine more categories.
  • Address and pin: enter the full, accurate address. If you don't meet customers at your address, you can choose not to show it. If you move after verification, you must verify again.
  • Service area: if you serve customers in a specific local area, set it by the cities, postcodes or other places you serve, as specifically and accurately as possible.
  • Business name: use the name as it appears on your real signage and branding. If you change the name after verification, you may need to verify again.
  • Review of changes: Google reviews your edits before they go live, so a change won't show instantly.

If you hide your address because you work at customers' premises, you'll appear differently from a business with a public shop. Check the service area still covers where your customers actually are.

6. The profile is marked as closed

Google's mark your business as closed page explains that a profile displays as Temporarily closed or Permanently closed on Maps and Search. A permanently closed profile may still appear for people who search for it, with the closure shown clearly. A temporarily closed profile can rank below open businesses for broad searches.

Google says to use Temporarily closed for closures of more than a week, for the off-season or for an indefinite closure, and to use special hours for shorter closures. If your profile was marked closed by mistake, or you reopened, the page says you can reopen the location. To check the status, go to your profile, select Edit profile, then Hours, and look at the business hours section.

Holiday closures are better handled with special hours than with a closed status. See setting holiday hours on Google.

7. It's on Maps, but you can't manage it

If your business appears on Maps but you can't edit it, someone else may own or manage the profile, or it may be unclaimed. The route depends on whether it's verified. Google's add or claim page lays out both.

The profile is unverified

Open Google Maps, search for your business name and town, select the claim option, then choose to manage it now, and pick a verification method. On the Google Maps app, open the business, go to About, and use the same claim option.

The profile is verified and owned by someone else

Use Request access. Google's page on requesting ownership gives the steps for a business with a shop or office:

  1. Go to business.google.com/addEnter your business name and address.
  2. Choose your business from the listA message says someone else may manage the profile.
  3. Select Request AccessIt's available if you're authorised to manage the business.
  4. Fill out the form and select SubmitYou'll get an email confirmation.

The current owner is notified by email and has three days to respond. If they approve, you get an email and can manage the profile. If they deny it, you can suggest an edit or appeal. If nobody responds after three days, you may be able to claim the profile: open the confirmation email, select View request, then Verify. Google notes this option isn't always available.

Service-area businesses with no shop or office don't use that form. Google tells them to use its Contact us page and enter "Transfer ownership of listing" as the issue.

It's common for the owner to be an ex-employee, a web designer or an agency that set the profile up years ago. If you know who it is, ask them directly to add you as an owner. Our guide to verifying your Google Business Profile covers the verification that follows a claim.

If none of this fixes it

If you've worked through every cause above and the profile still isn't showing, contact Google Business Profile support through the help centre and describe the symptom exactly: what you searched, from where, and what the profile status shows. Frequently asked questions

Why is my business not showing on Google Maps?

The usual causes are an unverified profile, a suspended or disabled profile, a listing treated as a duplicate, a closed status, or wrong category, address or service-area settings. Searching by name versus by service also gives very different results.

How long until a new Google Business Profile shows up?

Google says a verification review can take up to five business days, and sometimes longer. Some methods verify instantly, such as verifying your site in Search Console. Edits are also reviewed before going live.

Why can people find me by name but not by searching for my service?

That's a ranking issue. Google ranks local results on relevance, distance and prominence, and says you can't pay for a better position. Complete your profile, check your category and keep collecting and answering reviews.

Can a duplicate listing hide my profile?

Yes. Google says a profile it considers a duplicate won't show on Search or Maps. Search for your phone number and address, then request ownership, suggest a merge or remove your own duplicate.

My profile was suspended. Can I just make a new one?

No. Google says not to create a new profile for the same business. Fix the cause, then appeal in the appeals tool. An appeal can only be submitted once.

What does Request access do?

It asks the owner of a verified profile to give you access. They're emailed and have three days to respond. If they don't, you may be able to claim the profile, though Google says that option isn't always available.

Does being marked closed affect visibility?

Google says a temporarily closed profile can rank below open businesses in broad searches, and a permanently closed one may still appear for people searching for it. Use special hours for short closures.

Can I pay to appear higher on Google Maps?

No. Google says there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.
