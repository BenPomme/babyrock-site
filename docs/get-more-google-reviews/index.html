How to get more Google reviews

Find your review link, ask at the right moment, and stay on the right side of Google's rules and UK law.

Updated 25 November 2026 · By the BabyRock team

The short answer

To get more Google reviews, sign in to your Business Profile, select Read reviews, then Get more reviews, and copy your review link or save the QR code. Then ask every customer, soon after a good experience, in person or by message. Never offer a discount or gift in return, and never ask only the happy ones.

The steps

  1. Sign in on a computerOpen your Business Profile at business.google.com. Google says review QR codes can only be generated in a computer browser, not on a phone.
  2. Select Read reviewsThis opens the list of reviews your business has already received.
  3. Select Get more reviewsIt is the share icon. Google shows your review link and a QR code.
  4. Copy the link or save the QR codeUse the copy icon for the link. For the QR code, right-click it and choose Save image as.
  5. Put it where customers will see itOn receipts, in thank-you messages, at the end of a chat, on a printed card or poster. Google suggests exactly these places.

Get your Google review link

Google's help page on creating a link or QR code to request reviews gives the route in the steps above. The link opens the review form for your business directly, so the customer doesn't have to search for you, find the right listing and then hunt for the button. Every step you remove makes it more likely the review actually gets written.

Two practical points. Customers have to be signed in to a Google Account to leave a review, as Google's tips for getting more reviews say. Most people with an Android phone, or who use Gmail, already are, but some will stop at that screen, so don't take it personally if a few never post. And the same page says you can share the link by email, WhatsApp or a Facebook post, as well as printing the QR code.

Save the link somewhere you can find it in ten seconds: a note on your phone, a pinned message to yourself on WhatsApp, or the first line of a text template. If you have to log in to Google every time a customer says "how do I leave a review?", you will stop offering.

When to ask, and how

The best moment to ask is the moment the customer is visibly pleased and the memory is fresh: after the haircut is finished and they're looking in the mirror, when the boiler is running again, when they've just said "that was lovely". A request a week later competes with everything else in their life.

Ask everyone, not just the people you expect to be delighted. Google's content policy tells businesses not to "selectively solicit positive reviews". It also says businesses mustn't pressure customers on the premises or request specific content. Keep the request simple and open: "if you've got a minute, we'd really value an honest review on Google".

Copy-ready ask messages

Adapt these to your own voice and add the customer's name. Each one asks for an honest review, offers nothing in return and asks every customer, which is how Google wants it done.

Café

"Hi Priya, thanks for coming in today. If you enjoyed it, an honest review on Google would help other people find us, and it takes about a minute: (your review link). Thank you! Gareth, Mill Lane Coffee"

At the till: "If you've got a spare minute, there's a QR code by the card machine for a Google review. We read every one."

Barber

In the chair, once the cut is done: "Happy with that? If you fancy leaving an honest review on Google, the QR code's on the mirror. It really helps a small shop like ours."

By text the same evening: "Thanks for coming in, Dan. If you've got a minute, we'd love your honest feedback on Google: (your review link). Reece, Norwich Cuts"

Dentist

By email after an appointment: "Thank you for visiting us today. Your feedback helps us improve and helps other people choose a dentist they feel comfortable with. If you'd like to share your experience on Google, you can do it here: (your review link). Please don't include any health details in a public review."

Dental and other health practices should keep requests general and never reply to a review in a way that confirms someone was a patient.

Pub

Printed on the bill or on a table card: "Enjoyed your meal? Tell us on Google: scan the code. It helps us and the people deciding where to eat on Sunday."

To a group after a booking, by WhatsApp: "Thanks for celebrating with us on Saturday, Helen. If you'd like to leave an honest review of the evening, here's the link: (your review link). Marcus, The Crown"

What Google forbids

Google's Maps user contributed content policy is the rulebook. The parts that matter when you ask for reviews:

  • No incentives. Businesses may not offer payment, discounts, free goods or services in exchange for reviews. Google's help page on getting more reviews calls this strictly prohibited. That includes "leave us five stars and get a free coffee", and it includes a prize draw for everyone who leaves a review.
  • No review gating. You can't discourage negative reviews or selectively ask for positive ones. Sending the review link only to customers who said they were happy, and a private feedback form to everyone else, is selective solicitation.
  • No paying to change or remove a bad review. Google treats incentives to revise or remove a negative review as rating manipulation too.
  • No conflicts of interest. Reviews from current or former employees, family members or business partners are not allowed. Your mum, your staff and your supplier shouldn't be reviewing your shop.
  • No fake reviews. That means posting reviews yourself, asking friends to post reviews of experiences they haven't had, or using multiple accounts. Reviews must reflect a genuine experience.
  • No pressure on the premises or scripting. Don't tell people what to write, and don't stand over a customer's phone.

The risk is real. Google's help pages say fake engagement is removed, and breaking the rules can lead to restrictions on your Business Profile. A profile with reviews that vanish, or one that is restricted, is worse than one with a few honest reviews.

UK law: the CMA and fake reviews

In the UK, the Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) published guidance on fake reviews in April 2025, alongside a short guide for businesses. It explains what is banned under consumer protection law, and it is written for businesses that publish reviews.

The short guide describes fake reviews as reviews that look or claim to be genuine but aren't. It lists concealed incentivised reviews: where the reviewer received something in return, such as money or a free stay, and the review doesn't say so. Those are banned.

Note the gap between the two rule sets. UK law is aimed at fake and concealed-incentive reviews. Google's rules go further: it bans offering incentives at all, even if the reviewer says they got a free coffee. For a business owner, the practical rule is the stricter one, so don't offer anything in exchange for a review.

This is a plain-English summary of public guidance, not legal advice. If you run a business with a larger marketing operation, or a platform that publishes reviews, read the full CMA guidance or take advice.

Reply to every review you get

Asking is half the job. Google's tips for getting more reviews also say to reply to reviews, positive and negative, to show customers their feedback matters. People reading your profile see how you treat both sides. A short, personal reply to a good review and a calm, specific reply to a bad one tell a stranger more than the star rating does.

Making it a habit

Most owners don't have a review problem. They have a remembering problem. A simple routine beats a campaign:

  • Put the QR code in two places customers will actually look: the till or mirror, and the receipt or confirmation message.
  • Pick one person, ideally you, whose job it is to ask at the end of each visit.
  • Once a week, check new reviews and reply to them. Our guide to Google Business Profile management covers the rest of the routine checks.
  • Never count reviews as a target for staff, and never reward staff per review. It pushes people toward pressure, which Google's policy forbids.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get my Google review link?

Sign in to your Business Profile on a computer, select Read reviews, then Get more reviews, and copy the link or save the QR code. Google says review QR codes can't be generated from a mobile browser.

Can I offer a discount for a Google review?

No. Google's policy forbids offering payment, discounts, free goods or services in exchange for reviews, and treats such reviews as fake engagement that can be removed and can restrict your profile.

Is it allowed to ask only happy customers for a review?

No. Google's policy says businesses may not selectively solicit positive reviews or discourage negative ones. Ask every customer in the same way.

Can my staff or family review my business?

No. Google doesn't allow reviews written because of a conflict of interest, including current or former employees and family members.

Do customers need a Google account to leave a review?

Yes. Google says customers must be signed in to a Google Account to leave a review. They can create one with a non-Gmail email address.

What does UK law say about fake reviews?

The CMA's guidance says concealed incentivised reviews and fake reviews are banned under consumer protection law. Google's own rules are stricter on incentives, so don't offer any. This is not legal advice.

How many times should I ask a customer?

Once, plus at most one gentle reminder. Repeated chasing is annoying and can produce worse reviews.

Should I reply to reviews after I get them?

Yes. Google's own tips say replying to positive and negative reviews shows customers their feedback matters, and future customers read your replies.
