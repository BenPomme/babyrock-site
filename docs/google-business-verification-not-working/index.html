Google Business Profile verification not working: what to do

Code not arrived, verification failed, option missing or nothing happening: a fix for each, based on Google's own help pages.

Updated 25 November 2026 · By the BabyRock team

The short answer

If Google Business Profile verification isn't working, first identify which method you used. Postcards usually arrive within 14 days and expire after 30. Phone codes can't reach automated menus. A reappearing Get verified button means Google couldn't fully verify you, so try again or use another method. Don't edit your name, address or category while waiting.

The steps

  1. Find your methodOpen your profile and note whether you used postcard, phone, text, email, live video call or a video recording. Each fails in a different way.
  2. Check the basicsMake sure you can answer the phone or receive the text, can open the email address shown, and haven't changed your name, address or category.
  3. Match the symptomUse the table below: code not arrived, verification failed, option missing, or a long wait.
  4. Try another methodIf Get verified is back and Google offers more than one method, use a different one.
  5. Contact GoogleIf you've tried what's available and still can't verify, Google says to contact support.

What Google's help pages say, and what they don't

This page sticks to what Google states on its help pages, mainly Verify your business and Verify by video recording. Some of the problems people search for, such as "verification unavailable", a profile stuck on "pending", or greyed-out options, aren't described under those names. Where Google is silent, we say so and give the closest thing it does state, rather than inventing a fix.

Three facts from Google explain most of what goes wrong:

  • Google decides which methods you see. You can't change them. They depend on your business type, public information, region and opening hours, and some businesses need more than one method.
  • Not every method exists for every business. Google says live video verification may not be available to everyone, and neither is postcard verification.
  • Reviews take time. Google says verification reviews take up to five business days, and on rare occasions longer.

Postcard code not arrived

The postcard is the slowest method and the one people worry about most. Google says most codes arrive within 14 days, and that they expire after 30 days. So the safe window is a fortnight of waiting, and you must enter the five-digit code within a month of requesting it.

Three rules from Google's page apply while you wait:

  • Don't request a new code. Google says it invalidates the code already in the post and sends another one, which can make the process longer. The urge to press the button on day five is the most common way people slow themselves down.
  • Don't edit your business name, address or category. Google says that if you do, the code it sent you won't work. Hold any change until you're verified.
  • Don't share the code. Google says it will never ask you for it, and that you shouldn't share it with anyone, including people who manage your profile.

When the card does arrive, open your profile, enter the five-digit code in the Code field and select Submit. If 14 days have passed and nothing has come, check that the mailing address you confirmed was right and that the postcard hasn't been put aside with other post. If your profile offers another method, you can use it. If it doesn't, contact support.

Phone, text or email code not arrived

Google's page gives a simple rule for each of these, and it points at the receiving end rather than at Google.

  • Phone: you must be able to answer the business phone. Google says interactive voice response (IVR) systems won't receive the code. If your business line starts with "press 1 for...", the code call can't get through that menu. Use a line you answer yourself, then retry.
  • Text: you need to be able to receive texts on the number shown. A landline can't. Check which number Google is offering.
  • Email: you must be able to use the address shown on the verification screen. Open that inbox and look in your spam folder.

If the method you chose can't work for your setup, go back to Get verified and look for another. If there is only one, fix the cause (answer the line, get access to the inbox) and request again.

Verification failed, or Get verified keeps coming back

Google says that when the Get verified button appears again, it means it couldn't fully verify your business. Its advice is to verify again, and to try other methods if you're offered several. That is the whole instruction on that page, so there are no hidden extra steps.

For video verification specifically, a failure shows up as a Review issues notification. Select it and follow the on-screen instructions. Google's examples of why a video is rejected are a business name that isn't visible on the storefront, no nearby area shown, or no proof you're authorised to run the business. If Google asks for more details, it's likely because your business details recently changed. Select Get verified and verify again. If you edited your name, address or category recently, that's the likely cause, and it's one more reason to hold changes until the profile is verified.

"Verification unavailable", missing options and greyed-out buttons

Google's page doesn't use the phrase "verification unavailable", and it doesn't describe greyed-out options. What it does say is that the methods you see are chosen by Google, based on your business type, public information, region and business hours, and that you can't change them. It also says that live video verification and postcard verification aren't available for all businesses, and that live video calls only run within your location's business hours.

In practice, if the method you expected isn't there, the reasons Google gives are these:

  • Your business type doesn't qualify for that method.
  • Opening hours: a live video call is only possible when your location is open. Try again within your business hours.
  • Region: options vary by where the business is.
  • Eligibility: online-only businesses can't have a Business Profile, so they can't be verified at all.

Google's page doesn't say what to do if the Get verified button is missing altogether, so we can't give you a confirmed step for that. The closest confirmed advice is the last one on the page: if you can't verify after trying the available options, contact support.

Stuck waiting, or showing as pending

Google says verification reviews take up to five business days after you've completed the steps, and that on rare occasions they take longer. Google doesn't describe a separate "pending" status on its verification page, so the useful question is simply how long it has been.

  • Under five business days: wait. Don't resubmit, and don't change your business name, address or category.
  • Past five business days: open your profile. If Get verified has come back, Google couldn't fully verify you, so verify again or try another method.
  • Nothing changes after repeated attempts: contact support.

Remember that business days exclude weekends. A video submitted on a Friday evening is not late on Monday.

Don't edit your name or address while you wait

The one change Google explicitly warns about is for postcards: don't edit your business name, address or category, or the code won't work. Google doesn't say the same for every other method. But it also says that recently changed business details can lead to a request for more information, so the safe policy is the same for all of them: leave the core details alone until verification is done, then make your changes.

If your sign says one thing and your profile another, fix the mismatch after you're verified. For a video, the business name on your storefront has to match your profile name, so keep that consistent and don't tinker mid-process.

When to contact Google support

Google's verification page says to contact support if you can't verify after trying your options. It links to the Business Profile support form at support.google.com/business/gethelp. Before you go, it helps to have the following to hand, because support will ask what you've already tried:

  • Which method you used and when.
  • Whether a postcard was requested, and the date.
  • What you see on screen now: Get verified, Review issues, or nothing.
  • Whether you've changed your name, address or category since you started.

Google also recommends, for video verification that keeps failing, confirming the video meets every requirement and trying again, and trying another method before contacting support. Only use Google's own support route. Be wary of anyone who offers to verify your profile for a fee or asks for your code.

Frequently asked questions

How long should I wait for a Google verification postcard?

Google says most codes arrive within 14 days. Codes expire after 30 days, so enter yours within that time.

Should I request a new postcard if mine is late?

No. Google says requesting a new code invalidates the one already mailed and sends another, which can lengthen the process.

Why didn't I get a verification phone call?

Google says automated phone systems (IVR) won't receive the code. You must be able to answer the business phone yourself.

What does it mean when Get verified comes back?

Google couldn't fully verify the business. Verify again, and try other methods if you're offered more than one.

Why does Google want more information?

Google says it's likely because your business details recently changed. Select Get verified and verify again.

Can I edit my business details while waiting?

For postcards, Google says not to edit your name, address or category, or the code won't work. Leaving them alone until you're verified is the safe approach for every method.

How long does the review take?

Up to five business days, and on rare occasions longer, according to Google.

How do I contact Google about verification?

If you can't verify after trying the available options, use the Business Profile support form linked from Google's verification page, at support.google.com/business/gethelp.
