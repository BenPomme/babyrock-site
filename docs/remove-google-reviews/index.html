How to remove Google reviews

The honest answer: you can't delete a review, but you can report one that breaks Google's rules. Here is what qualifies, how to report it and what to do if it stays.

Updated 25 November 2026 · By the BabyRock team

The short answer

You can't delete a Google review yourself. You can report it, and Google removes it only if it breaks its content policy, for example spam, fake engagement, profanity, harassment or personal information. Disagreeing with a review isn't enough. Report from your Business Profile, track the result in the Reviews Management Tool, and appeal once if it's refused.

The steps

  1. Check it breaks a ruleCompare the review with Google's list of prohibited and restricted content. A bad, unfair or unwelcome review that describes a real experience is usually not enough.
  2. Report it from your Business ProfileOpen your profile, choose Read reviews, select Report next to the review and pick the reason that fits best. Then send the report.
  3. Track the decisionGoogle says evaluation typically takes several days. Check the status in the Reviews Management Tool: decision pending, reviewed with no violation found, or escalated.
  4. Appeal once if refusedIf Google finds no violation and you disagree, you can submit a one-time appeal for the review in the same tool.
  5. Reply and earn new reviewsIf it stays, reply calmly in public and keep collecting genuine reviews from happy customers. That is what moves your overall rating.

The honest answer first

There is no button that deletes a review from your Business Profile. Reviews belong to the people who wrote them. What you can do is report a review to Google. Google then checks it against its content policies and removes it only if it violates them.

Google is clear about the limits. Its help page says you can report any review, but only those that violate its policies are eligible for removal. It also says not to report a review just because you disagree with it or dislike it, and that Google doesn't get involved in conflict between businesses and customers. See Report inappropriate reviews on your Business Profile.

Anyone who tells you they can guarantee the removal of a genuine review is either mistaken or selling something. We come back to that below. First, what actually qualifies.

Which reviews does Google remove?

Google's Prohibited and restricted content policy for Maps sets the rules. In plain English, these are the areas most relevant to a business owner:

Policy areaWhat Google's policy saysWhat it looks like in practice
Fake engagementContributions must reflect a genuine experience. Content not based on a real experience, paid-for reviews and content posted from multiple accounts by or at the request of one person are not allowed.A review from someone who never visited, or several reviews from accounts that appear to be one person.
Conflict of interestContent based on a conflict of interest, such as current or former employment, a contractual relationship or being an industry competitor, is not allowed.A sacked employee or a rival business posting a one-star review.
Unusual patternsContent showing unusual volumes or patterns of reviews that suggest an attempt to manipulate a place's rating is not allowed.A sudden burst of near-identical one-star reviews in a short window.
Harassment and hate speechSpecific threats of harm, doxxing and content that vilifies people for a protected characteristic are not allowed.A review threatening your staff or naming a member of staff in an abusive way.
Personal informationPosting someone's personal information without consent, such as a full name, a face in a photo or medical or financial information, is not allowed.A review that publishes a staff member's home details or a full name and a photo of them.
Offensive content and profanityAttacks on individuals, clearly provocative content, unsubstantiated allegations of unethical behaviour or criminal wrongdoing and profanity used to offend are not allowed.A review accusing you of a crime with no evidence, or one filled with abuse.
MisrepresentationFalse or misleading accounts of a good or service, or distorting or omitting facts, are not allowed.A review describing a service you don't provide.
Off-topic and advertisingContent should be based on an experience at the place. General, political or social commentary, personal rants and advertising or solicitation are not allowed.An advert for another company, or a rant about an unrelated subject.

Google adds that it does allow content that describes negative experiences in a respectful manner. A review that is critical, unflattering or simply wrong about a detail, but describes something the writer really experienced, is generally not a policy violation. Those you answer, not report. Our guide to responding to negative Google reviews shows how.

Reviews you can't get removed this way

  • A harsh but genuine review. Fair or not, it stays.
  • A review you think is inaccurate. Unless it is a misrepresentation that breaks the policy, you can reply with the facts but the review remains.
  • A one-star rating with no text. There is nothing for Google to assess against its text rules. Reply politely if you wish.
  • Third-party reviews. Your profile may show reviews from other sources. See Google's page on third-party reviews for how those are handled.

How to report a review to Google

You need to be a verified manager of the profile. If you haven't verified yet, start with how to verify your Google Business Profile. Then there are two routes, both described in Google's help.

From your Business Profile (Search or Maps)

  1. Go to your Business ProfileSign in with the Google account that manages the business and open your profile, either on Google Search or in Google Maps.
  2. Select Read reviewsThis shows all your reviews.
  3. Select Report next to the reviewFind the review you want to flag and choose Report.
  4. Choose the reasonPick the one that fits, such as spam or profanity. Choose the reason that best matches what is actually wrong, because the report is assessed against the policy.
  5. Select Send reportGoogle reviews it. Evaluation typically takes several days.

With the Reviews Management Tool

Google also has a dedicated Reviews Management Tool. In summary, as set out on Google's help page:

  • Open the Reviews Management Tool and confirm the email address linked to your Business Profile, or switch account if you manage the profile with a different one.
  • Select your business, then choose to report a new review for removal.
  • Next to the review, select Report, choose the reason in the new tab and submit.
  • Merchants in the EEA can report third-party reviews directly on Google, according to the help page.

The exact labels in Google's screens change from time to time, so follow what you see on screen alongside the current Google help page.

Reporting the person instead of the review

If a user profile contributes inappropriate content, you can report the profile too. Google's help describes doing this in the Google Maps app on your mobile device: tap Business, then Reviews, tap the user's name, then More and Report, and pick a reason such as legal issue or privacy concerns. More detail is in Report inappropriate user profiles.

Make your report easy to assess

  • Report each review separately and pick the most accurate reason.
  • Don't report a review because it hurts. Report it because it matches a line in the policy.
  • Keep your own record: a screenshot, the date, and anything in your systems showing no matching customer. You don't always need it, but it helps if you need to appeal or talk to Google support later.

Checking the status and appealing

You can check what happened to a report by returning to the Reviews Management Tool. Google lists these statuses:

Status in the toolWhat it meansWhat to do
Decision pendingThe review is flagged but hasn't been evaluated yet.Wait. Don't submit the same report again and again.
Report reviewed, no policy violationGoogle evaluated it and found no violation.If you disagree, you can submit a one-time appeal.
Escalated, check your email for updatesThe appeal has been escalated and you'll get an email about the final decision.Watch the email linked to the profile.

To appeal, go to the Reviews Management Tool, confirm your email, select your business, choose to check the status of a review you reported earlier, then select Appeal eligible reviews. You can choose up to 10 reviews, then continue and fill out the form. When it is assessed, you get an email: if the review violates the policy it is removed, and if it complies it stays live.

The appeal is one-time. Use it for a case where you have a clear policy reason, not as a second try because you are annoyed. It helps to match your appeal to the policy wording. For example, state that the reviewer was never a customer and had a conflict of interest, not just that the review is untrue.

Reviews that vanish or are delayed

Sometimes a review disappears without your doing anything. Google says it uses automated spam detection to remove reviews identified as spam, and that occasionally the system may mistakenly remove legitimate reviews. If a genuine review of yours has gone missing, see About missing or delayed reviews.

Special cases

Fake reviews

If you think a review comes from someone who never used your business, a competitor or a former employee, you are in the area of fake engagement and conflict of interest. Our guide to fake Google reviews covers how to spot and evidence them, and how to reply without accusing anyone in public.

Someone is demanding money to remove reviews

If you receive a message asking for payment, gifts or favours to stop or remove negative reviews, don't pay and don't negotiate. Google has a separate page for this: Report negative review extortion scams on your Business Profile. Report it there and keep the messages as evidence.

Business Profile restrictions

Google can restrict a profile for policy violations, and that includes review-related violations. If your own profile has review features switched off or limited, read Google's Business Profile restrictions for policy violations.

What not to do

  • Don't pay anyone to remove reviews. There is no paid route to removal. Services offering guaranteed removal of genuine reviews are either selling something they can't deliver or breaking the rules. Google's policy specifically prohibits merchants from offering incentives in exchange for the revision or removal of a negative review, and that applies to you as well as to customers.
  • Don't offer the customer anything to change their review. Not a refund, a discount or a free item. Fix the problem on its own merits and keep it separate from the review.
  • Don't ask friends, family or staff to flood in five-star reviews. Content posted at your request that doesn't reflect a genuine experience is exactly what Google's policy prohibits, and it makes your profile look worse, not better.
  • Don't report every review you dislike. Google says not to report a review just because you disagree with it, and a report that is plainly a disagreement is unlikely to succeed.
  • Don't argue in public. A reply that attacks the reviewer does more damage than the review.

If the review stays up

Most negative reviews stay. That isn't the end of the story. What readers see is the complaint, your reply and the rest of the profile around it. Two jobs matter now.

1. Reply well

Google tells owners that replying shows customers you value their feedback, and that the reviewer is notified and can change their review. A calm, specific reply helps every future reader. Use our guide to responding to negative Google reviews, or start from the response templates.

2. Keep earning genuine reviews

The only lasting way to push a bad review down is a steady flow of honest reviews from customers who were happy. Google allows you to ask. It doesn't allow you to pay for them, pressure people or only ask the ones you expect to be positive. Also answer the rest. Profiles that reply to everyone, good and bad, look run by someone who cares. See how to respond to Google reviews for the whole routine.

Frequently asked questions

Can I delete a Google review of my business?

No. Only the person who wrote it can delete it. You can report a review to Google, and Google removes it only if it violates its content policy. You can also reply publicly.

How long does Google take to remove a reported review?

Google says review evaluation typically takes several days. You can see the current status in the Reviews Management Tool: decision pending, reviewed with no violation found, or escalated after an appeal.

What happens if Google says there is no violation?

You can submit a one-time appeal in the Reviews Management Tool, for up to 10 eligible reviews at a time. Google emails the result. If the review complies with its policies it stays live.

Will Google remove a review just because it is unfair?

Not necessarily. Google says not to report a review just because you disagree with it, and it allows content describing negative experiences in a respectful manner. It removes reviews that break its policy, such as fake engagement, harassment, profanity or personal information.

Can I pay someone to remove a bad Google review?

No. There is no paid way to have a genuine review removed, and a promise of one should be treated with suspicion. Google's policy also bans merchants from offering incentives in exchange for removing a negative review.

Can a customer remove or change their own review?

Yes. A customer can change their review after reading your reply, and the date on it updates to show the latest change. Fixing their problem and replying calmly is how you give them a reason to.

What if someone asks me for money to take reviews down?

Don't pay. Google has a dedicated page for reporting negative review extortion scams on your Business Profile. Keep the messages and report them there.
